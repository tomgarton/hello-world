Motion direction

What to settle before opening Rive: when she reacts, how her tail shows feeling, how snappy she is, and a few moves taken from a real toddler.

Rules
Never in the way. Nothing waits for an animation. Saving, navigating and typing are instant; she reacts alongside.
No guilt. She is never sad, worried or disappointed about what a parent did or didn’t log. Sad is only for her own stories.
Quiet at night. After dark she is smaller, slower and stays on the ground.
Silent by default. No sound unless switched on. A soft haptic tap when she lands.
One thing at a time. When she moves, the scene behind her goes still.
She notices you. Her eyes follow your finger, and she glances at what you just pressed.
2. The tail shows the feeling
She has no mouth or brows, so the tail is her mood signal, with her eyes. One position per feeling.
Calm
Resting, slightly raised. The default.
Happy
Up and wagging. Small marks show the wag in stills.
Proud
Straight up, like a flag.
Surprised
Stiff and straight out.
Sad
Droops to the ground. Only for her own feelings, never about the parent.
Shy
Tucked in close, half hidden.
Sleepy
Curled around her feet.
3. Smooth or snappy
The same two moves in two timings. Smooth eases all the way; snappy holds each pose and pops to the next.
Hooray, smooth
Eased the whole way. Soft, a little floaty.
Hooray, snappy
Holds each pose, pops to the next. Bouncier, more toddler.
Wave, smooth
Arm swings continuously.
Wave, snappy
Clear up, tilt, up, tilt. Reads faster.
My pick: snappy for reactions (wave, hooray, nod), smooth for anything ambient (breathing, sleeping, scenes). Snappy reads faster at a glance and feels like a small child; smooth keeps the background calm.
4. Moves from a real toddler
Key poses in a three-quarter view. Each is one clear silhouette to animate into and hold.
Three-quarter, at rest
Her acting view. The bill and one cheek turn towards us; movement reads more naturally than front-on.
Potty dance
Knees together, hands at the front of her dress, tail stiff. A quick side-to-side jiggle. The app can use it as the gentle "time to try?" reminder.
Flop-sit
Legs shoot out, bottom lands, arms fly up for balance, soles to camera. For finishing something, or giving up happily.
Tippy-toe reach
Up on her toes, one arm stretched, the other out for balance, tail as counterweight, eyes on the prize. For "add" and "not yet" moments.
