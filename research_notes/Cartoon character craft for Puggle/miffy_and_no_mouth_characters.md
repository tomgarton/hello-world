## 1. Dick Bruna's rules in the books, and how Miffy changed from 1955

### Takeaway
Bruna's Miffy is built from a hand-painted black contour with a slightly soft, shaky edge, flat fills from a small fixed palette, no perspective, and a face of two dots and a small cross that looks straight at the reader. Emotion comes from very small changes (eye spacing and angle, a slight head tilt, a single tear), reached by throwing away many attempts. The 1955 Miffy was a different, floppier design that did not meet the reader's eye; the familiar look dates from the 1963 square-format books.

### Cited Findings
Line and process
- Bruna sketched in pencil on tracing/transparent paper; the sketch line is described as "a slightly shaky, natural line". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- He traced the drawing onto watercolour paper with a hard pencil and then "painted the contours with black acrylic poster paint". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- A second account of the same step: the lines were scored through onto soft paper and the groove filled with black poster paint, which gives a slightly blurred line; the black outlines were then transferred to transparent film and laid over different coloured collages to choose colours. (snippet only) — [Rijksmuseum, design stages for "miffy's letter", 2003](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Design-Stages-for-miffys-letter--5933fb1e43bda61371f431b6f31f5929)
- From the early 1960s he used a Matisse-style "drawing with scissors" method: printers supplied cut sheets in a bespoke Bruna palette, and he overlaid cut-outs under the line film "until he found the right fit". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- He would "throw out hundreds of attempts"; perfecting one image could take weeks; "Each time I draw Miffy I feel nervous". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- Images were refined over weeks, reduced and erased until little remained; his wife Irene was his strictest critic and a book that did not move her was redone. — [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)
- Bruna's stated aim was reduction to the essence, with no redundant line. (snippet only) — [DesignCurial, "the art of reduction"](https://old.designcurial.com/news/miffy-creator-dick-bruna---the-art-of-reduction-270115-4497133/)

Colour
- Wikipedia gives a nine-colour palette: black, white, red, yellow, blue, green, orange, brown, grey. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- Another source lists primaries (red, yellow, blue) plus green and orange with black and white. (snippet only; differs from the nine-colour list, which adds brown and grey) — [Tokyo Opera City Art Gallery](https://operacity.jp/ag/exh190/e/exh02.php)
- The claim that the primary colours are what make her recognisable to preschoolers is tagged "citation needed" on Wikipedia. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)

Face, gaze, emotion
- "With two dots and a little cross I have to make her happy", or a little happy, cross or sad. — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- Emotion is carried by "a minute tilt of the head, a blink of the eye". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- Bruna said his figures are always facing you, describing a child seeming to stand at his drawing table looking at him. (snippet only) — [Malay Mail / AFP obituary](https://www.malaymail.com/news/read/2017/02/18/dick-bruna-dutch-creator-of-miffy-the-rabbit-dies-at-89/1317603)
- Bruna was careful not to draw his characters in profile. (snippet only; student newspaper, secondary) — [Oxford Student](https://www.oxfordstudent.com/2017/02/26/childhood-memories-remembering-dick-bruna/)
- On sadness, to the Guardian (2006): "At the end I have one big tear, and that is the saddest tear you can have." — quoted in [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)
- Because the expression is blank, small shifts in alignment change the emotional effect, and the simplicity leaves room for readers to project feelings. — [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)

Page and book format
- Each Miffy book: 12 story pages, one illustration per page, four lines of verse with lines two and four rhyming; everyday situations; always a happy ending; small format aimed at the child. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- Small square books resembling a window; four lines of text on the left, one bold picture on the right. — [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)
- "Lack of perspective"; Miffy is related to rooms and landscapes rather than placed in depth. — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)

Change over the decades
- 1955: rectangular-format book; Miffy looked "like a flat cuddly toy", ears "flopped to the side", eyes "not yet communicating so directly with the reader". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- The 1950s Miffy had wonky ears, askew feet and "refused to look the reader in the eye". — [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)
- By 1963 the design had become the stylised rabbit; in 1970 Bruna decided Miffy was a girl because he wanted to draw a dress. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- Later: "her head became more round", ears "sharper and more symmetrical", "the eyes bigger and wider apart". — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)

### Inferences
- The "trembling" quality is a by-product of the method (brush on absorbent or scored paper), not a wobble added on top. For Puggle the equivalent is one fixed, slightly irregular stroke profile baked into each part, not a per-frame boil. A boiling outline would read as nervous, which is the opposite of Miffy's calm.
- The direction of travel over 60 years was toward more symmetry, rounder head, wider-set and larger eyes, and direct gaze. Those are the levers that make the face "communicate", and they are all available to a platypus toddler.
- Bruna's emotional vocabulary is tiny: eye position and spacing, head tilt, a blink or part-closed eye, one large tear, ear angle. Puggle has more channels (eyebrows, cheeks), so the risk is over-use; Bruna's practice suggests using one device per beat.
- Flat staging without perspective means the "set" can be a few coloured shapes; depth is implied by overlap and size only.

### Gaps
- I could not open miffy.com's "the black lines" page or the Telegraph interview, so the widely repeated statements that Bruna said the line should have "a heartbeat", that a brush was specifically chosen to make it tremble, and that the eyes/mouth were moved by fractions of a millimetre are NOT verified here. I recall them from general reading, but treat them as unsourced until checked at https://www.miffy.com/the-black-lines.
- No source found on ear position as an emotion device in the books (I believe drooping ears are used for sadness in some titles, unverified).
- No source found giving exact proportions (head-to-body ratio, eye spacing).
- The palette count differs between sources (seven versus nine colours); I could not find a Mercis statement to settle it.

## 2. Earlier TV and the stop-motion series "Miffy and Friends" (2003)

### Takeaway
Before CGI, Miffy on screen was a 1992 traditionally animated series of five-minute storybook episodes directed by Gene Deitch, then a stop-motion puppet series (2003) in which the characters never speak and a single female narrator tells the story, as a book being read aloud. Production credits and end dates for the 2003 series are inconsistent between sources.

### Cited Findings
- 1992: "Dick Bruna's Miffy Storybook Classics", traditionally animated, about five minutes per episode, directed by Gene Deitch. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- 1996: "Miffy: Colors, Numbers, and Shapes" is listed but not described. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- "Miffy and Friends" is a Dutch stop-motion series "presented in a storybook style"; narration explains the actions of "non-speaking Miffy and her friends". Narrators: Cyd Vandenberg (US/Canada), Karin Hollreiser (UK/Australia/NZ). — [Wikipedia: Miffy and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_and_Friends)
- 39 episodes of two segments (78 segments), three seasons; first aired on KRO 3 February 2003, to 30 March 2005; directors listed as Raymond Jafelice and Andrew Young; co-produced by Mercis Media and Palm Plus Multimedia, also credited to Big Tent Entertainment and Mercis BV. — [Wikipedia: Miffy and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_and_Friends)
- Conflict: the main Miffy article gives 2003–2007 and says it was produced by Pedri Animation BV, a Dutch stop-motion company. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)
- Pedri Animation's own site lists "Miffy and Friends" among its series and describes itself as a builder of stop-motion puppets. (snippet only) — [Pedri Animation](https://www.pedri-animation.com/about.html)
- Reception: Common Sense Media calls it a gentle show for the youngest viewers. (snippet only) — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/tv-reviews/miffy-and-friends)
- An IMDb user review says it looks like clay stop-motion; another praises soft colours and simple design. (snippet only; user reviews) — [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1614180/)
- Mercis producer Frank Padberg later referred to the "warmth and beautiful atmosphere of the previous stop motion series". (snippet only) — [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/entertainment/miffy-heads-sonys-tiny-pop)

### Inferences
- The most likely reading of the credit conflict is that Pedri did the puppet animation as service studio while Mercis and partners were the producers. Not confirmed.
- The decisive adaptation choice in 2003 was structural, not visual: keep the characters silent and put all language in a narrator. That preserves the picture-book relation (an adult voice reads, the child looks at a still-faced character) and removes the need for lip-sync on a face whose mouth is a fixed cross.
- For Puggle this is the cheapest and most faithful model: narrator or sound cues carry meaning, the character acts in pantomime.

### Gaps
- No source found on how the puppets were built (whether flattened to look 2D), whether a black outline was reproduced (I believe the puppets had no outline and were soft matte solids, unverified), how head turns and walking were handled, or frame rate.
- No source on Gene Deitch's 1992 approach beyond the basic credits.
- Studio 100's catalogue lists "Miffy specials" from 2005 and 2007 as 3D CGI (snippet only, [Studio 100](https://www.studio100international.com/en/catalog/miffy-specials/)), which would conflict with the 2015 series being described as Miffy's first move into CGI. Unresolved.

## 3. "Miffy the Movie" (2013), Blue-Zoo's CGI series (2015), and the 2025 CGI series

### Takeaway
The film and both later series moved steadily away from the book conventions: the film has Miffy speak and address the audience; the Blue-Zoo series gives every character a voice actor while keeping a narrator and seven-minute stories; the 2025 Superprod series is described by its makers as the "same iconic look" with a "contemporary" world, a new sibling and more character-driven emotional stories. I found no technical statement from Blue-Zoo about outline, head turns, mouth animation or frame rate.

### Cited Findings
Miffy the Movie (Nijntje de film)
- Released 30 January 2013 in the Netherlands; directed and edited by Hans Perk; produced by KRO and A. Film; described as a stop-motion family film, with puppet animation filmed in Latvia. (snippet only) — [Wikipedia: Miffy the Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_the_Movie)
- Trade press also calls it "the 2013 stop-motion family pic". (snippet only) — [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=205209)
- A Dutch review gave 4/5, praised it as a Bruna book brought to life, and noted that Miffy speaks to young viewers and invites them to join a treasure hunt. (snippet only) — [FilmVandaag](https://www.filmvandaag.nl/nieuws/1678-recensie-nijntje-de-film)
- Won Best Dutch Film at Cinekid. (snippet only) — [Wikipedia: Miffy the Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_the_Movie)

Miffy's Adventures Big and Small
- CGI series by Blue-Zoo (London) with Mercis BV; announced as Miffy's first CGI series; production began April 2014; premiered 2 October 2015; ran to 2019; seven-minute segments. — [Wikipedia: Miffy's Adventures Big and Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy%27s_Adventures_Big_and_Small)

- Episode totals on that page do not reconcile (104 episodes versus three seasons; 52 then 26 segments ordered). — [Wikipedia: Miffy's Adventures Big and Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy%27s_Adventures_Big_and_Small)
- Maker statement, Adam Shaw: the series "will embrace the charm of Dick Bruna's artwork". (snippet only) — [C21 Media](https://www.c21media.net/news/miffy-gets-cgi-reboot/)
- Maker statement, Frank Padberg (Mercis): Blue-Zoo recreated "the same warmth and beautiful atmosphere" of the stop-motion series. (snippet only) — [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/entertainment/miffy-heads-sonys-tiny-pop)
- Mercis's Marja Kerkhof said Blue-Zoo was chosen for its understanding of the brand. (snippet only) — [TV Kids / WorldScreen](https://worldscreen.com/tvkids/?p=10239)
- Distributor description: a world "in the simple iconic style of Dick Bruna". (snippet only; promotional) — [Studio 100](https://www.studio100international.com/en/catalog/miffys-adventures-big-and-small/)
- Blue-Zoo published a storyboard training video for Miffy using PanelForge (a combined 2D/3D boarding tool). (snippet only) — [Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/221642062)

2025 series ("Miffy", StudioCanal / Mercis / Superprod)
- 3D CG, 78 episodes of about seven minutes, for ages 3–5, made at Superprod Animation, announced October 2023 for Canal+ in 2025; UK on Sky Kids. — [AWN](https://www.awn.com/news/studiocanal-mercis-superprod-team-miffy-3dcg-preschool-series); [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=205209)
- Maker statements: Miffy keeps "the same iconic look" with "a fresh and contemporary vision"; StudioCanal's Françoise Guyonnet says the team will "fully respect the legacy"; Superprod's Clément Calvet and Jérémie Fajner stress keeping her "positive attitude" and sense of adventure. — [AWN](https://www.awn.com/news/studiocanal-mercis-superprod-team-miffy-3dcg-preschool-series)
- Content changes: new younger brother Bun; mother works outside the home, father at home; more character-driven stories with emotional lessons. — [AWN](https://www.awn.com/news/studiocanal-mercis-superprod-team-miffy-3dcg-preschool-series)
- No stop-motion or 2D Miffy production from the 2020s turned up; the new series is CGI. — [Licensing Source](https://licensingsource.net/new-cgi-animated-miffy-series-to-launch-in-2025)

### Inferences
- Across 1992 → 2003 → 2013 → 2015 → 2025 the pattern is: first the image is kept and the storybook form is kept (narrator, silence); then the voice is added; then the storytelling becomes "character-driven". The look is the last thing the rights holder lets go of; the silence is the first.
- The maker statements available are all about "charm", "warmth" and "iconic look", which suggests the brand owner's stated rules concern recognisability (silhouette, palette, face) more than animation technique.
- A dedicated 2D/3D storyboard tool on the Blue-Zoo show hints that staging was planned in flat, readable compositions before animation, which fits a design that only reads well from a few angles. This is a guess from a single video title.

### Gaps
- No Blue-Zoo case study, director interview or trade article was found that explains: how the 3D model handled the black outline (I believe the CGI series drops the outline and uses soft matte, felt-like shading, unverified); whether the head snaps between fixed views or turns freely; whether the cross mouth is animated for speech (I recall it flexing/opening during dialogue, unverified); blinking; frame rate or stepped animation. All of these should be checked by watching episodes directly, which I could not do in this session.
- Whether "Miffy the Movie" is true puppet stop-motion or CGI styled to look like it: the sources I saw say stop-motion with puppet work in Latvia, but I only saw summaries and A. Film is mainly known as a drawn/CG studio. Treat as probable, not certain.
- No information on directors or visual rules of the 2025 series, and no reviews of it were retrieved.

## 5. Comparable characters and the devices they use

### Takeaway
Mouthless or near-blank characters split into two strategies: keep the face still and let the viewer project (Hello Kitty in print, Miffy in the books, Rilakkuma), or keep the face simple but make the body, sound and added graphic marks do loud work (Pingu, Molang, Pocoyo). Animation has repeatedly pushed blank-faced characters toward getting a mouth or a voice.

### Cited Findings
Hello Kitty
- Original designer Yuko Shimizu: "I couldn't express the mouth in a cute way, so I decided not to use it." — [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japan/hello-kitty-face/)
- Lead designer Yuko Yamaguchi: the blank face lets people "project their own feelings"; "Kitty looks happy when people are happy. She looks sad when they are sad." — [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japan/hello-kitty-face/)
- Sanrio lines: she "speaks from the heart" and is not bound to any language. — [Wikipedia: Hello Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Kitty); [SoraNews24](https://soranews24.com/2014/08/28/hello-kitty-isnt-a-cat-we-called-sanrio-to-find-out/)
- Yamaguchi also said Kitty "has a mouth" that is "hidden in the fur" (Wired, 1999). — [Wikipedia: Hello Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Kitty)
- In animation she was given a visible mouth, from "Hello Kitty's Furry Tale Theater" (1987). (snippet only) — [Wikipedia: Hello Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Kitty)
- The New York Times (2010) reported analysts saw the missing mouth as a handicap for her as an animated TV character. — [Wikipedia: Hello Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Kitty)
- Voiced animated series continued (1989–94 OVAs, 2008 CGI series, a 2018 vlog format where she talks to camera, 2020 "Supercute Adventures" in 2D). — [Wikipedia: Hello Kitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Kitty)
- Bruna told the Daily Telegraph he thought Hello Kitty "is a copy" of Miffy; Mercis won a 2010 Dutch ruling against Sanrio over the rabbit Cathy, settled in 2011. — [Wikipedia: Miffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy)

Pingu
- Created by Otmar Gutmann with Erika Brueggemann; clay stop-motion; an invented babble language instead of dialogue. — [Wikipedia: Pingu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingu)
- Summaries and reviewers say the lack of words pushes storytelling onto action, vocal inflection and body language. (snippet only) — [Wikipedia: Pingu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingu)
- A fan wiki says clay was used for arm and head movement and replacement parts for mouth and legs. (snippet only; fan wiki) — [Pingu Wiki](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/w:c:pingu%3APingu_%28TV_series%29)

Molang
- Designed by Hye-Ji Yoon as chat stickers; animated by Millimages; characters speak only invented "Molanguese", which Millimages says made the show universal and easy to export. — [Molang official history](https://www.molang.com/molang-history)
- Style borrows anime marks: sweat drops and special eye shapes for strong emotion; oversized heads on short simple bodies. (snippet only) — [Wikiwand: Molang](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Molang)

Rilakkuma
- "Rilakkuma and Kaoru" (Netflix, 2019): stop-motion by dwarf studios, directed by Masahito Kobayashi; shot on about ten stages at roughly ten seconds of footage per session. (snippet only) — [Wikipedia: Rilakkuma and Kaoru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rilakkuma_and_Kaoru)

Pocoyo
- 3D characters on a plain white background, usually with no backdrop. — [Wikipedia: Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)
- Co-creator Guillermo García Carsí, on the newer production: "There are things we couldn't do before in that white background". (snippet only) — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/events/pocoyo-creator-case-study-animaj-ai-255463.html)

Moomin
- For "Moominvalley" (Gutsy Animations, 3D), studios pitching for the work were asked to convey Moomintroll's personality through movement and body language. (snippet only) — [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=105997)

### Inferences
- Device inventory, by channel, that these characters rely on:
  - Eyes: spacing, size, blink, half-closed lids, replacing dots with a shape (arc, cross, spiral) for extreme states (Molang, anime convention).
  - Marks borrowed from comics: sweat drop, tear, blush patches, motion lines, a question or exclamation mark (Molang; Bruna's single tear).
  - Body: whole-body squash and stretch, lean, bounce, slump; ears or tail as mood indicators (Miffy's ears, Moomin's tail and ears).
  - Sound: babble with clear intonation (Pingu, Molang), or a narrator who names the feeling (Miffy 2003, Pocoyo).
  - Staging: empty or near-empty background so that a small pose change is the largest thing on screen (Pocoyo, Bruna's pages).
  - Props and context: the tear, the dropped ice cream, the object looked at.
- Shimizu's remark is a useful design test: if a mouth cannot be drawn in the character's style without spoiling it, leave it off and move expression elsewhere. That is already Puggle's position.
- The Hello Kitty history is a warning. Once a mouthless character must carry dialogue, studios tend to add a mouth, and the character stops being the same design. Avoiding lip-synced dialogue avoids the problem entirely.
- Puggle's bill is a large fixed feature where a mouth would be. Pingu's beak (which stretches into a trumpet shape when he calls) shows a beak can be expressive, but that is a high-energy, elastic character; for a calm Miffy-like one, small bill tilts with the head are the safer reading. This is my judgement, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No maker interview was found for Pingu, Molang, Rilakkuma, Pocoyo or Moomin that explains expression rules directly; items above are from encyclopaedia summaries. In particular, I could not verify how Rilakkuma's face is handled in stop-motion (I believe his face stays fixed and acting is done with head angle, body and Kaoru's reactions, unverified), whether the Moominvalley Moomins show a mouth when speaking (I believe the mouth appears under the snout only when talking, unverified), or Pocoyo's timing principles.
- No Sanrio primary page was opened; the Sanrio quotes come through Wikipedia, Tofugu and SoraNews24.

## 6. General lessons for a frontal-only flat design on a simple part-based rig

### Takeaway
None of the sources states animation rules for flat frontal characters, so this section is almost entirely inference from the evidence above. The consistent thread is: keep the face toward the viewer, change very little at a time, hold poses, and let narration, sound and a few graphic marks carry meaning.

### Cited Findings
- Bruna's figures face the reader and avoid profile. (snippet only) — [Malay Mail / AFP](https://www.malaymail.com/news/read/2017/02/18/dick-bruna-dutch-creator-of-miffy-the-rabbit-dies-at-89/1317603); [Oxford Student](https://www.oxfordstudent.com/2017/02/26/childhood-memories-remembering-dick-bruna/)
- His pictures have no perspective. — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/the-making-of-an-illustrator-dick-bruna-s-miffy-1)
- Small shifts in alignment change the emotional effect of a blank face. — [The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/dale-shaw-rampant-rabbit-the-simple-joy-and-complex-roots-of-dick-brunas-miffy/)
- The 2003 series kept characters silent and used one narrator. — [Wikipedia: Miffy and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_and_Friends)
- Pocoyo isolates characters on plain white. — [Wikipedia: Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)

### Inferences
Turning in space
- Treat the frontal view as the home pose and the only pose held for any length of time. For movement across the screen, the body can face the direction of travel while the head stays frontal or three-quarter-cheated toward the viewer (the book convention of body sideways, face forward).
- If a side or back view is ever needed, snap to it in one or two frames rather than rotating; a smooth turn exposes in-between shapes the design never had. Limit the set to front, a slight left/right cheat (features slide a few pixels across the head, head outline unchanged), and back.
- "Looking" at something can be done without turning: slide both eye dots a small distance toward the target and tilt the head. This is the Bruna-scale device and suits a part-based rig (eyes as separate parts on the head).

Outline in motion
- Keep stroke width constant in screen space; do not scale parts non-uniformly in ways that thin or fatten the line. Where parts overlap (arm over body), decide once whether the outline is drawn per part or merged, and keep it that way, since joints are where a part rig breaks the Bruna look.
- Bake the hand-made irregularity into the artwork. If any line "life" is wanted, a very slow, low-amplitude swap between two or three drawn versions at long intervals is the most that fits the character; constant boil does not.
- Squash and stretch should be mild, so the outline shape stays recognisably the same silhouette.

How much movement
- Favour held poses with small secondary motion (a blink, a breath, an ear or tail settle) over continuous motion. The books give one image per thought; the nearest animated equivalent is pose-to-pose with clear holds.
- One expressive change per beat: eyes OR brows OR cheeks OR body, then hold. Reserve stacked changes (tear plus slump plus brows) for the story's single biggest moment, following Bruna's "one big tear".
- Emotional range can be mapped to millimetre-scale parameters on the rig: eye spacing and height, eyelid/blink state, brow angle and height, cheek blush opacity, head tilt, body lean, bounce height. Test each alone on the neutral face before combining.
- Keep backgrounds flat and sparse so small changes register.
- Prefer narrator, music and non-verbal sounds to speech; if Puggle ever "talks", let the body bob or the head nod in rhythm rather than adding a mouth.

### Gaps
