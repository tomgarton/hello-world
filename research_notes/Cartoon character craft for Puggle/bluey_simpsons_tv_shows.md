# Bluey, The Simpsons and simple-design TV cartoons: design rules and animation methods (for Puggle)

Confidence labels used below: **[crew]** = said by someone who made the show, in an interview or their own writing; **[trade]** = trade press or reference article reporting; **[wiki]** = Wikipedia or fan wiki, lower confidence; **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result summary, page not read in full, lowest confidence.

## 1. Bluey (Ludo Studio, Brisbane): character design, animation method, acting, backgrounds

### Takeaway
Bluey's whole world is built from one shape idea, the big rounded rectangle ("rounded cube"), with characters locked first and everything else designed to match them. The show is rigged 2D in CelAction, composited in After Effects over backgrounds that are painted in Photoshop under a written style bible: no black line, coloured shadows, flattened perspective and a wide flat floor for acting.

### Cited Findings

Character design and shape language
- [crew] Art director Costa Kassab describes the look as "chunky" with a "rounded-cube 'shape language'" built on parallel lines and simplified forms, and says the environment style is "quite caricatured to harmonise with our simple characters". — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [crew] Kassab says each new character is a design job of finding a new dog breed and stylising it into that same shape language. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [crew] Catriona Drummond (lead art director, series 1) says the characters were already locked when she started, so backgrounds had to be built to suit them; she designed "anything that wasn't the characters". — [Creating Bluey, chapter 1 (Drummond's Substack)](https://goodsniff.substack.com/p/creating-bluey-tales-from-the-art)
- [crew] Drummond's shape-language primer from the style bible: circles read friendly and soft, triangles sharp and aggressive, squares sturdy and grounded; horizontals calm, verticals alert, diagonals unbalanced. Bluey's world uses big rounded rectangles to match the characters, carried down to details such as cupboard knobs. — [Creating Bluey, chapter 2](https://goodsniff.substack.com/p/creating-bluey-tales-from-the-art-891); also [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [crew] Style bible rule for props: "Keep objects cute, simplified and chunky, with doll house proportions." — [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [crew] Style bible rule: no literal dog gags (no bone-shaped doors, no kennels); only subtle nods. — [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [trade, from a licensed game team that worked with Ludo] Gameloft's artists say the characters look simple but their shapes, proportions, expressions and poses are extremely specific, and that outlines keep "a consistent thickness regardless of the distance of the object from the camera". — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)
- [trade] The same team notes the show uses straight-on (0 degree) and full-profile (90 degree) character views sparingly, favouring the angles in between. — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)

Animation method
- [crew] Joe Brumm says the studio animates in CelAction, which he learned in the UK and which is also used on Peppa Pig and Charlie and Lola. Pipeline: script, then design and backgrounds, then layout, then animation; a team of about 50 to 60 in one Brisbane studio; four teams of five animators per episode. — [World Screen / TV Kids Festival report](https://worldscreen.com/tvkids/?p=22314) (read via search summary only, [snippet])
- [snippet] Ludo's own behind-the-scenes post says final character drawings are turned into rigs with controls on faces and joints that animators use for movement and expression. Page returned 403, so wording is unverified. — [bluey.tv blog](https://www.bluey.tv/blog/bluey-behind-the-scenes)
- [trade] CelAction2D is marketed as the software behind Bluey, Peppa Pig and Mr. Bean. — [Animation Magazine, 2023](https://animationmagazine.net/2023/09/bluey-software-celaction2d-launches-version-4-1)
- [crew] Rich Jeffery (director) says scenes are output as separate layers (background, characters, foreground) and recombined in After Effects; there is no colour grade because final colour is painted up front in Photoshop; storyboards are hand-drawn on Cintiq; slow motion is simply animated slower by hand. Each episode takes roughly three to four months with a dozen or more in production at once. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [trade] Gameloft's animators say they used the series' smear frames as reference and worked with Ludo to learn how its 2D rig worked and the "rules" for animating in the Bluey style. This confirms the show uses smears and a documented rule set, but the rules themselves are not published. — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)

Staging, camera and acting
- [crew] Brumm: "I wanted to avoid making a quick, 'cutty' show" because young viewers are still learning film grammar; longer shots give the audience time to track characters. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [crew] Jeffery: the show is mostly flat, with characters usually on a single plane; wide shots establish geography first, then "we go close in" on anything the audience must not miss. Depth of field is faked by blurring layers. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [trade] Gameloft's animation lead names the qualities they had to match from the show: "weight, line of action and appeal", with movement kept clear and readable. — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)

Backgrounds, colour and light
- [crew] Style bible: "Do not use black line-art"; line is coloured according to the local object and the light on it. Note this means Bluey backgrounds do have line, just coloured line. — [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [crew] Style bible: "Avoid neutral shadow tones"; err towards more colourful lighting; if unsure, push the shadow cooler or warmer than the object's own colour. Light is a simplified, slightly more vivid Brisbane light: golden, with cool blue shadows. — [Creating Bluey, chapter 2](https://goodsniff.substack.com/p/creating-bluey-tales-from-the-art-891)
- [crew] Perspective: backgrounds read as one-point with flat, camera-facing planes, but are built as two-point with vanishing points either side of the back wall. The purpose is a wide, level ground plane for flat, graphic character staging. — [Creating Bluey, chapter 2](https://goodsniff.substack.com/p/creating-bluey-tales-from-the-art-891)
- [crew] Readability rule illustrated in the style bible: do not let a character half-cover a key prop; the prop the scene is about must be clearly visible. — [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [crew] Drummond: "at a foundational level they are paintings"; she painted outdoors around Brisbane to capture light. Kassab: the team keeps "purposeful colour schemes" per location and mood, and simplifies objects by asking which shapes to remove or add so a tree still reads as its species. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [crew] Brumm's brief to Drummond was that the show be "beautiful" and "enthralling and enticing to look at"; her test for a style included whether it could be reproduced by a crew on budget and schedule, hence the written style bible. — [Creating Bluey, chapter 1](https://goodsniff.substack.com/p/creating-bluey-tales-from-the-art)
- [trade] Gameloft's rebuild of the look: flat swatch-colour bases with hand-painted detail; value and saturation used to push things back or pull focus; detail pared back for readability; no dynamic lighting, and characters do not react to lighting changes. — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)

### Inferences
- For Puggle, the most transferable Bluey rule is "one shape family for everything, and the characters come first". Pick Puggle's base shape (a rounded form consistent with a Miffy-like head) and make props, furniture and scenery echo it at "doll house" scale.
- Bluey separates characters from backgrounds by treatment, not just colour: characters are flat fills with a consistent-weight outline and no lighting response; backgrounds carry the light, colour temperature and painterly detail. Puggle's dark-outlined characters over outline-free flat-shape backgrounds is an even stronger version of the same split, so it should read well provided background value and saturation are kept quieter than the characters.
- A wide, level floor band and mostly side-on, single-plane staging suits a simple part rig and is what Bluey deliberately engineers its perspective to provide.
- Since Bluey avoids pure front and pure profile views, a three-quarter default view may give more appeal. This conflicts with a Miffy-style front-facing design, so it is a choice to make deliberately, not a rule to copy.
- Coloured shadows (never grey) and a per-scene colour scheme tied to mood are cheap to apply to flat-shape backgrounds.

### Gaps
- No crew source found that states how the Heeler characters themselves were designed (who drew them, why rectangular torsos, how markings and colour distinguish family members, why no everyday clothes). That Bluey is blue, Bingo red-orange, and that outlines are a darker shade of the fur colour is visible on screen but I found no crew statement on the reasoning.
- No crew source found describing how much of Bluey is rig versus drawn, or how eyes, brows, ears and tails are used for acting. The only evidence is indirect (rig face controls [snippet]; smear frames and unpublished "rules" via Gameloft). No interview with an animation director was found.
- The Costa Kassab podcast episode ("Master of the Blueyverse", Bluey's Brisbane podcast) and Drummond's Substack chapters 3 and 4 were located but not read; they may hold more detail.
- Team size and animation-team figures come from a search summary of the World Screen report, not the page itself.

## 2. The Simpsons: Groening's design rules, model sheets and staying on model

### Takeaway
The Simpsons' two founding design goals were silhouette recognisability and designs simple enough that expressions are easy to change; consistency across hundreds of artists is then enforced by an exhaustive style guide that specifies construction down to counted hair points. Much of the widely quoted "rules" detail circulates second-hand and should be treated with care.

### Cited Findings
- [wiki, citing Groening's DVD commentaries for seasons 1 and 6] The five family members were given simple designs so their facial emotions could be changed with little effort and so they would be recognisable in silhouette. — [Wikipedia, The Simpsons (franchise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_(franchise))
- [wiki, citing a David Silverman interview] The yellow skin was chosen by colourist Gyorgyi Peluce, not by Groening. — [Wikipedia, The Simpsons (franchise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_(franchise))
- [wiki, citing BBC documentary and Silverman] Groening's first drawings were rough sketches he expected to be cleaned up; the animators traced them, which fixed the crude look. — [Wikipedia, The Simpsons (franchise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_(franchise))
- [crew, via trade] Former showrunner Josh Weinstein shared pages from a "nearly 500-page early style guide, circa 1990". The pages shown cover Lisa: eye placement defined relative to the nose; the nose may overlap the eyes; specific smile construction. — [SlashFilm, April 2020](https://www.slashfilm.com/573760/the-simpsons-style-guide/)
- [crew, via trade] Same guide: Lisa's hair is always "eight points of hair clustered in groups of two or three" from any angle; her neck line always runs into the body and arm lines, with rules on which lines hide behind the arm. — [SlashFilm, April 2020](https://www.slashfilm.com/573760/the-simpsons-style-guide/)
- [snippet] A former production assistant says the guide gives each character an orthographic view, extreme poses and facial expressions. — [HowStuffWorks](https://entertainment.howstuffworks.com/the-simpsons.htm)
- [snippet, second-hand summary of a video about the guide] Characters may be wall-eyed but never cross-eyed; pupil-to-eye diameter ratio of 7:1. Unverified: the page returned 403 and the ratio's direction is ambiguous as quoted. — [MetaFilter thread](https://metafilter.com/199570/Im-Bart-Simpson-who-the-hell-is-THAT)
- [snippet] Creative Bloq and Uproxx covered the same leaked guide pages, including the shape of Lisa's teeth and a "what you will never see" list of forbidden drawings. Not read in full. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/simpsons-style-guide); [Uproxx](https://uproxx.com/tv/the-simpsons-style-guide-what-you-will-never-see)

### Inferences
- The lesson for a cast is to write rules as countable, checkable facts ("eight points, in groups of two or three", "eyes sit here relative to the nose") instead of adjectives. For a code-driven rig this maps directly onto constants: eye spacing as a fraction of head width, brow travel limits, cheek position.
- A "never" list is as useful as a "do" list. For Puggle that could include: never add a mouth, never cross the eyes, never let outline weight vary, never rotate the head beyond a set angle.
- Silhouette test: fill every cast member solid black at thumbnail size; each should be identifiable by head shape, ears, bill or tail alone. Simpsons hair does this job; for Australian animals the equivalent is ears, snout or bill, tail and one accessory.
- "Simple so expressions are easy to change" supports keeping Puggle's face to very few parts, each with a small set of clean states.

### Gaps
- No first-hand Groening quotation was retrieved for the silhouette rule, the overbite, bulging eyes or hair; the silhouette claim rests on Wikipedia's citation of DVD commentaries. No reliable source at all was found for an overbite rule. Several how-to-draw sites state specifics (head-height counts, hair point counts for Bart) but look like content farms and were excluded.
- The full style guide has never been officially published; only isolated pages shared on social media are public.
- Nothing sourced on the show's limited palette beyond the yellow-skin origin.

## 3. Other simple-design shows: Peppa Pig, Hey Duggee, Sarah & Duck, Pocoyo

### Takeaway
Each of these shows picks one strong simplification (child's-drawing profiles, pure geometry, restrained colour with accents, a blank white world) and then spends the saved effort on performance. The makers consistently say simplicity was also a production decision: fewer details means faster animation, easier consistency and clearer group shots.

### Cited Findings

Peppa Pig (Astley Baker Davies)
- [wiki, citing the studio's own page] Animated in CelAction2D. Characters blush when embarrassed, a simple cheek-based emotion cue. — [Wikipedia, Peppa Pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peppa_Pig)
- [trade] A 2004 CelAction course at the studio was taught by a member of the show's crew, who noted that Peppa's arm colour is changed to suit different backgrounds so the thin limbs stay visible. — [Skwigly, 2004](https://www.skwigly.co.uk/peppa-pig-celaction-2d-and-the-future-of-british-animation/)
- [snippet] The flat style is described as mimicking how young children draw, and the profile-with-both-eyes face is said to allow a full range of expression in side view; producer Phil Davies recalls funders wanting 3D-friendly designs for toys. Sources not read in full. — [Kidscreen feature](https://kidscreen.com/?p=215618); [Guardian feature via inkl, 403](https://www.inkl.com/news/it-was-like-meeting-the-pope-how-peppa-pig-became-a-1bn-global-phenomenon)

Hey Duggee (Studio AKA, Grant Orchard)
- [crew] Orchard started from basic geometric shapes, as in his earlier rectangle-only films, "only this time we threw in some eyeballs and mouths". — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [crew] The show is made in Flash, not After Effects or CelAction: "we decided to use no outlines and no effects outside of what Flash could do", using preset symbols so style stays consistent and render time is negligible. This contradicts the assumption in the brief. — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [crew] Detail was stripped because six main characters often share a shot, and extra detail "makes things quite congested and confusing for the kids". — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [crew] "'Reuse' isn't a dirty word on the show": good animation is reused so effort goes into new performance. About 16 people in-house on one floor. — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [crew] Duggee, the most detailed character (gradients, badges, tie), is the hardest to animate because small shifts in facial feature position push him "off-model". — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [snippet] Press coverage notes some characters are barely more than a shape with eyes (the frogs are triangles), which speeds animation. — [Yahoo/Telegraph feature](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/hey-duggee-cult-cbeebies-show-090027390.html)

Sarah & Duck (Karrot)
- [crew] Co-creator Sarah Gomes Harris set strict colour rules, "only using strong colours like Red in moderation as accent colours"; textures come from photographed patterns (a holiday mosaic became the hedge texture). — [Skwigly feature](https://skwigly.co.uk/sarah-duck)
- [crew] Producer Jamie Badminton: "the stronger the character animation, the more the emotional investment"; children respond to truthful performance. The concept was chosen as "just simple enough" to make entirely in the London studio. — [Skwigly feature](https://skwigly.co.uk/sarah-duck)
- [snippet] Animated in CelAction2D, with PNG sequences composited in After Effects. — [Cartoon Brew review](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/tv/sarah-and-duck-tv-review-84860.html); [ProVideo Coalition (studio-sourced)](https://www.provideocoalition.com/karrot-animation-expands-2d-possibilities/)

Pocoyo (Zinkia; Guillermo García Carsí, David Cantolla, Luis Gallego)
- [wiki] 3D in Softimage XSI on a plain white background with usually no backdrop; early episodes were animated pose-to-pose, unlike the fluid motion of most 3D shows. — [Wikipedia, Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)
- [wiki] Colour is assigned one per character: Pocoyo all blue, Pato a yellow duck with a small green hat, Elly pink, Loula orange, Pulpo red, Nina green. Each character also has a signature sound and dance. — [Wikipedia, Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)
- [crew, snippet] García Carsí on the white void's limits: "There are things we couldn't do before in that white background", such as shadows and forests. — [Cartoon Brew event report](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/events/pocoyo-creator-case-study-animaj-ai-255463.html)

### Inferences
- Peppa's blush and Hey Duggee's shape-plus-eyes characters show that a mouthless or near-featureless face can carry emotion if cheeks, eyes and whole-body pose do the work. Puggle's cheeks are a legitimate primary emotion channel (blush on, blush bigger, blush colour shift).
- Orchard's off-model warning applies directly: on a very simple face, a pixel or two of drift in eye position changes the character. Lock eye and brow positions as ratios of the head and limit how far expressions may move them.
- Peppa's arm-colour trick is a practical fix for thin limbs vanishing against a background: allow per-scene limb or outline tweaks, or check each scene's background values against the characters.
- Pocoyo's pose-to-pose style (strong pose, fast move, hold) is the natural fit for a part-based rig with no in-between drawing.
- Sarah & Duck's accent-colour rule suits flat outline-free backgrounds: keep scenery muted and reserve the strongest hues for characters and the one prop that matters.
- Build a reuse library early (walk, hop, wave, sit, surprise) and treat reuse as a feature, as Hey Duggee does.

### Gaps
- No first-hand quote from Astley, Baker or Davies on Peppa's profile design was read in full; treat the children's-drawing and both-eyes-in-profile rationale as widely reported but unverified here.
- No maker statement found on why Pocoyo uses a white background; the usual explanation (focus on characters, avoid overwhelming preschoolers) appeared only on fan pages.
- Nothing found on how Duck's wordless acting is achieved in Sarah & Duck, nor on Hey Duggee's comic timing methods beyond Orchard's general remarks. The Pictoplasma talk and Design Museum "Designing Duggee" material were located but not read.
- Miffy (Dick Bruna), the closest design relative of Puggle, was outside this brief and not researched.

## 4. Techniques for making simple cut-out rigs feel alive

### Takeaway
Sourced practitioner advice found here is limited to one good article: keep the design simple, let the rig do subtle movement and moving holds, use drawn or swapped frames for the big showy moments, and animate on twos so the result has a drawn texture instead of a smooth puppet glide. The rest of the standard toolkit is listed under Inferences as general animation practice, not sourced findings.

### Cited Findings
- [trade, practitioner] Chris Cray (animator/director): design joints as circles, because rig parts rotate about fixed points and circular joints hide the seam. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade, practitioner] Simplify the design as if every frame were hand-drawn; excess detail draws the eye to parts that are not moving and makes the character harder to keep on model. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade, practitioner] Use the rig for minute, subtle motion and moving holds, since computed in-betweens are steadier than hand drawing; use drawn frames for the "intuitive and the impossible" such as smears and morphs. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade, practitioner] When mixing, the frame where rig and drawing meet should be a rig pose, to avoid a visible pop. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade, practitioner] Put everything on twos (each image held two frames at 24 or 25 fps); stripping computed in-betweens down to twos gives rigged motion a traditional texture. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade, practitioner] Nest swap or frame-by-frame sequences inside rig parts (face, hands) so expressive features can change drawing while still travelling with the puppet. — [Motionographer, 2023](https://motionographer.com/2023/03/21/7-ways-to-mix-frame-by-frame-and-rigged-animation)
- [trade] Bluey uses smear frames within its rigged pipeline (Gameloft used them as reference). — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)
- [wiki] Pocoyo's early episodes are pose-to-pose instead of continuously fluid. — [Wikipedia, Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)
- [crew] Hey Duggee reuses good animation deliberately. — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)

### Inferences
The following are standard animation practice applied to Puggle's rig; they are my synthesis, not statements from the sources above.
- "Floaty puppet" motion comes from even, linear or slow ease-in-out interpolation on every part at once. Counter it with: fast transitions (roughly 2 to 6 frames) between clearly different poses, then a hold; asymmetric easing (quick out, soft settle); and parts arriving at slightly different times.
- Anticipation and settle: a small move opposite to the action before it (squash down before a hop), then overshoot the final pose by a few percent and ease back.
- Moving holds: during a held pose keep something alive at low amplitude, such as a slow breathing scale on the body, an occasional blink, a tail or bill drift. On iOS this is cheap to do procedurally, layered over keyed poses.
- Eye and brow swaps: change eye shape and brow angle as discrete states, switching in one or two frames, usually timed on a blink or at the fastest point of a head move so the swap is hidden.
- Secondary motion: ears, tail, hair tuft or accessory lag the body by a few frames and overshoot. A spring or damped follow driven by the parent's motion does this automatically.
- Smear or swap drawings: for very fast moves, insert a single stretched shape for one frame, or apply squash and stretch along the direction of travel, instead of interpolating the rig.
- Stepping to twos: sampling the rig at 12 fps for body poses while keeping camera moves and procedural breathing smooth is a way to test the "on twos" texture in an app; whether it suits a 60 or 120 Hz touch interface is untested.
- With no mouth, the blink, brow, cheek and head tilt carry dialogue beats; a small head bob or body bounce on syllables can stand in for lip-sync.

### Gaps
- No studio-authored source (Ludo, Astley Baker Davies, Karrot, CelAction case study) was found describing timing charts, hold lengths, or specific methods for avoiding puppet-like motion. Specific frame counts above are rules of thumb, not sourced.
- The Animated Conversations podcast with Andy Blazdell of CelAction was located but not listened to.
- The "12 principles" origin (Thomas and Johnston, The Illusion of Life) underlies several inferences but was not consulted directly.

## 5. Handling a cast: size, silhouette, colour, accessories, staging

### Takeaway
The shows keep casts readable with one shared construction plus one or two strong differentiators per character (species silhouette, a single assigned colour, a signature accessory), and they simplify further as the number of characters on screen rises.

### Cited Findings
- [wiki, citing Groening commentaries] Every Simpson family member is designed to be recognisable in silhouette. — [Wikipedia, The Simpsons (franchise)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_(franchise))
- [crew] Bluey: new characters are made by picking a different breed and stylising it into the same rounded-cube shape language, so the cast shares construction and differs by breed features and colouring. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/)
- [wiki] Pocoyo: one dominant colour per character, plus an accessory (Pato's green hat) and a signature sound and dance each. — [Wikipedia, Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)
- [crew] Hey Duggee: with six main characters in shot, detail is removed to avoid congestion; screen time is rotated fairly ("hasn't had much to do recently"); characters are not coded by gender. — [Cartoon Brew interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/?p=149069)
- [crew] Bluey staging: characters mostly on one plane on a wide flat floor; establish geography wide, then cut close for important detail; few cuts so young viewers can track who is where; do not let a character obscure a key prop. — [Australian Cinematographer magazine, 2021](https://acmag.com.au/2021/09/01/bluey/); [It's Nice That, 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/how-we-built-bluey-s-world-cartoon-background-scenery-art-director-catriona-drummond-animation-090725)
- [trade] Bluey's rig shares hands, feet and mouths across characters (as rebuilt by Gameloft from the show's assets), so common parts are identical cast-wide. — [80.lv, Gameloft interview](https://80.lv/articles/bluey-s-simple-2d-look-required-gameloft-s-most-technically-challenging-art-pipeline)
- [trade] Peppa Pig adjusts a character's limb colour to keep it visible against particular backgrounds. — [Skwigly, 2004](https://www.skwigly.co.uk/peppa-pig-celaction-2d-and-the-future-of-british-animation/)

### Inferences
- For Puggle's Australian animal cast: one shared body template and shared hands/feet/eye parts, then differentiate by (1) species silhouette feature (bill, ears, snout, tail, spines, pouch), (2) one owned body colour, (3) at most one accessory.
