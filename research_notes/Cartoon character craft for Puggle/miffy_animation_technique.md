# Miffy (Nijntje) on screen: animation technique and what the makers kept or changed

Provenance labels used below:
- **[Maker]** = statement by someone who made or commissioned the work
- **[Fact]** = production fact from a page I read in full
- **[Snippet]** = seen only in a search-result summary; the page itself was not read (blocked, or not fetched)
- **[Critic]** = reviewer's observation
- **[Inference]** = my own reasoning

Overall caveat: detailed technical write-ups (rig breakdowns, puppet construction notes, style guides) for Miffy productions are very scarce online. Most of what exists is trade announcements. The few solid technique facts are marked clearly; several of the specific questions asked (painted outlines on puppets, replacement faces, toon-line rendering, blinking) could not be answered from any source and are listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

---

## 1. "Miffy and Friends" / Nijntje en haar vriendjes (2003, stop-motion)

### Takeaway
Pedri Animation (Netherlands), a puppet-building stop-motion studio founded by ex-Toonder Studios people, made the series as commissioned work at a rate of about five minutes of animation per week with roughly twenty people. The characters do not speak and a narrator carries the story; no source found describes how the puppets' faces, outlines or head turns were built.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker]** Pedri Animation lists "Miffy and Friends" as one of its two best-known productions and describes itself as a stop-motion studio focused on building animation puppets (puppet, clay, cut-out, pixilation). — [Pedri Animation, About](https://www.pedri-animation.com/about.html) (read via search summary of the page; the credit itself is a maker's self-description)
- **[Maker]** In September 2006 project manager Thomas Hietbrink said Pedri was working on a series of Nijntje episodes, distributed in 54 countries and made "puur in opdracht" (purely as commissioned work). — [AV & Entertainment Magazine, Sept 2006, "Stop-motion animatie volop in beweging"](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/382/Stop-motion-animatie-volop-in-beweging) (PDF read in full)
- **[Maker]** Scale and speed, same interview: on top of five permanent staff, "een man of vijftien extra" worked on it; 78 five-minute episodes had already been made earlier; output was "vijf minuten in de week". — [AV & Entertainment, 2006](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/382/Stop-motion-animatie-volop-in-beweging)
- **[Maker]** Pedri shot fully digitally (digital still cameras, up to 4K) and did compositing in house: enlarging landscapes, adding camera moves afterwards, and painting out rods ("stangetjes en dergelijke wegwerken"). This is said of the studio's general pipeline in 2006, while Nijntje was in production, not of Nijntje specifically. — [AV & Entertainment, 2006](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/382/Stop-motion-animatie-volop-in-beweging)
- **[Maker]** Director Peter Mansfelt (co-director of Pedri with Paul Mathot; founders trained at the Toonder studios) said the quality of animation depends on "de kwaliteit van de poppen, de manier waarop ze kunnen bewegen". — [AV & Entertainment, 2006](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/382/Stop-motion-animatie-volop-in-beweging)
- **[Maker]** In July 2009 Hietbrink said Pedri was developing a way to place computer-generated faces on its stop-motion puppets, to get more expression more efficiently "zonder de stopmotionfeeling" being lost. He presents this as a new project in 2009, which implies it was not the method used on the 2003-2006 Nijntje episodes. — [AV & Entertainment, July 2009, "Ontwikkelingen genoeg op stop-motiongebied"](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/555/-Ontwikkelingen-genoeg-op-stop-motiongebied-) (PDF read in full)
- **[Fact, Wikipedia, uncorroborated]** English Wikipedia credits directors Raymond Jafelice and Andrew Young, companies Mercis Media, Palm Plus Multimedia and Big Tent Entertainment, 39 half-hours (78 segments), and narration that explains the actions of the "non-speaking Miffy and her friends"; it calls the show "presented in a storybook style". It does not name Pedri, any animator, or any puppet detail, and gives no source for these credits. — [Wikipedia, Miffy and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_and_Friends)
- **[Snippet]** A Dutch library DVD description says the puppets move fluidly, use simple colours, and that puppets and sets alike are made in rounded forms. — [Bibliotheek Sint-Katelijne-Waver catalogue](https://sint-katelijne-waver.bibliotheek.be/catalogus/dick-bruna/nijntje-knorretje-en-andere-vriendjes/dvd/library-marc-vlacc_2733034?branch=Antwerpen%2FSint-Katelijne-Waver%2FSchool+Heilig+Hart)
- **[Critic]** A 2013 film reviewer contrasts the TV series with the books: the series is "driedimensionaal" and not "plat als in de boeken van Bruna". — [FilmTotaal, Arjan Welles, 29 Jan 2013](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Maker, about the successor show]** Mercis described the 2015 CGI series as keeping the "warmth and beautiful atmosphere" of the earlier stop-motion series, i.e. Mercis regarded the stop-motion look as the benchmark. — **[Snippet]** [Animation Magazine, MIPCOM 2014](https://www.animationmagazine.net/2014/10/mipcom-2014-quelque-chose-de-cartoons/)

### Inferences
- The numbers fit together: 78 five-minute films at five minutes per week is about a year and a half of shooting per batch; the 2006 batch was a later run on top of those 78. The "39 episodes / 78 segments" on Wikipedia matches the "78 afleveringen van vijf minuten" Pedri cites.
- Because the characters never speak and a narrator tells the story, the series needed no lip-sync at all. That is the cheapest and most Bruna-faithful answer to "what does the cross-mouth do": nothing. This is directly transferable to Puggle (no mouth, narrated or sound-effect driven).
- Pedri's 2009 remark that CG faces on puppets were a new development suggests the Nijntje puppets had physically fixed or physically swapped faces, but which of the two is not documented.

### Gaps
- No source found states whether the black outline was painted on the puppets, whether faces were flat discs, painted or replacement parts, how head turns were handled while keeping the face frontal, how walking was done, or the frame rate (ones or twos). I looked for Pedri, Mercis, KRO and Dutch press accounts and found none describing puppet construction.
- No statement found from Pedri's animators about Bruna's approval of the puppets. The New York Times piece "For Young Viewers; Dutch Bunnies with Depth" (2004), cited on Wikipedia, is the most likely place for this but could not be fetched.
- Wikipedia's director credits (Jafelice, Young) are unsourced and I could not corroborate them.
- A Pedri page about a "clay look" series of 40 episodes for NTR's "Het Zandkasteel" surfaced in search; that appears to be a different rabbit property (Konijn/Konijntje), not Nijntje, and is excluded.

## 2. "Miffy the Movie" / Nijntje de film (2013)

### Takeaway
It is real stop-motion puppet animation (not CGI imitating it), combined with digital backgrounds and effects, directed by Hans Perk of A. Film. The characters have voice actors, but a reviewer reports that the faces barely move: only one character's mouth (Grunty / Knorretje) moves, and only to show emotion, not to lip-sync.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact]** Dutch Wikipedia calls it a "stop-frame" puppet animation film, directed by Hans Perk, with production companies Telescreen Filmproducties, A. Film A/S and AB Studio Latvia; premiered 30 January 2013. Production credits are unsourced on the page. — [nl.wikipedia, Nijntje de film](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijntje_de_film)
- **[Fact]** English Wikipedia: stop motion; KRO, Telescreen, A. Film A/S, AB Studio Latvia, Mercis BV; Perk directed and edited; Dutch and English voice casts; "animated in the style of" Miffy and Friends. Its only source for the technique is a festival listing. — [Wikipedia, Miffy the Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy_the_Movie)
- **[Critic]** Technique as described in a review: stop-motion figures "aangevuld met digitale achtergronden en speciale effecten". — [FilmTotaal, 29 Jan 2013](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Mouth and face: "alleen het mondje" of Knorretje (voiced by Marc-Marie Huijbregts) moves, "en dan nog alleen om emoties uit te drukken". The reviewer says adults may find the static faces odd but the target audience probably will not notice. — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Fidelity: "Perks animaties zijn in lijn met de oorspronkelijke tekeningen" of Bruna; the figures are simple and recognisable; Perk did not follow the stereoscopic 3D trend. — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Reuse: animation for the recurring songs is repeated ("gecopypaste") at the start and end of each task. — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Another review credits the stop-motion choice as working "absoluut in het voordeel van de film" and says Bruna's cheerful primary colours come across well; the film invites children to talk back to the screen. — [Cinemagazine](https://cinemagazine.nl/nijntje-de-film-2013-recensie/)
- **[Snippet]** One search summary says the puppets were built in the Netherlands at Pedri Animation and animated in Denmark at A. Film. — source page not identified with certainty; surfaced alongside [nl.wikipedia, Nijntje en haar vriendjes](https://nl.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijntje_en_haar_vriendjes)
- **[Snippet]** Another search summary says the puppet animation "was filmed in Latvia", and that figures were moved by hand with each small change photographed. — surfaced from [cinema.nl](https://www.cinema.nl/db/9738741-nijntje-de-film) / [Nederlandse Filmdatabase](https://www.nederlandsefilmdatabase.nl/nederlandse_film.php?id=1201)
- **[Fact]** The FilmTotaal still carries an image credit that appears to read "Animācijas Brigāde", the Riga puppet-animation studio, consistent with the "AB Studio Latvia" credit. — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)

### Inferences
- The two snippets conflict on where the shoot happened (Denmark versus Latvia). The Latvian credit appears in both Wikipedias and in a stills credit, so the most likely reading is: A. Film (Denmark) as Perk's studio and co-producer, puppet shoot at AB / Animācijas Brigāde in Riga. Pedri's role as puppet builder rests on one unverified snippet.
- The film shows the limit case for a no-mouth or cross-mouth character with a voice cast: voices play over essentially static faces, and expression comes from body, ears and staging. One critic flagged it as noticeable to adults only.

### Gaps
- No interview with Hans Perk on technique was found. Dutch Wikipedia cites a Neerlands Filmdoek interview (29 Jan 2013) that may contain it; not located.
- Whether Miffy's own cross-mouth ever changes shape in the film is not stated by any source; the review only says Knorretje's mouth is the one that moves.
- Budget, frame rate and puppet construction: not found. The Dutch animation blogs that covered the trailer (animatieblog.nl, animatie.blog.nl) were unreachable.

## 3. "Miffy's Adventures Big and Small" (2015, Blue-Zoo with Mercis)

### Takeaway
The one hard technique fact: Blue-Zoo animated it in Maya but deliberately on twos and threes so that it would "feel like stop motion", i.e. the CGI imitates the 2003 puppet series rather than the flat book page. Nothing public describes the outline, face construction, mouth or blink rules.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker, reported second-hand]** Blue-Zoo co-founder Tom Box, in a talk at the Blue GFX Expo (2015) as written up by an animation tutor: the series was animated in Maya but made to "feel like stop motion", and animators worked on 2s and 3s so the motion felt less smooth. — [Animation Apprentice blog, Nov 2015](https://animationapprentice.blogspot.com/2015/11/blue-zoo-explains-challenges-of-running.html)
- **[Maker, reported second-hand]** Same talk, rendering: Mental Ray was too slow; Arnold looked good but took around 15 minutes a frame; the studio switched to the GPU renderer Redshift partway through production and the client did not notice the change. — [Animation Apprentice blog](https://animationapprentice.blogspot.com/2015/11/blue-zoo-explains-challenges-of-running.html)
- **[Maker]** A Blue-Zoo lighting and rendering artist confirms Maya, Arnold then Redshift, a small team across 52 episodes, and that "particular attention to detail" was needed to fit the visual aesthetic of the source material. — [Leo Tsang portfolio](https://leotsang.co.uk/Miffy.html)
- **[Maker]** Blue-Zoo director Adam Shaw at the announcement: the CGI series would "embrace the charm of Dick Bruna's artwork". Mercis described the tone as warm and engaging, based on universal childhood experiences. — [C21Media, "Miffy gets CGI reboot", 9 April 2014](https://c21media.net/miffy-gets-cgi-reboot)
- **[Fact]** Directors credited: Adam Shaw, Helen Arntsen, Chris Drew (Wikipedia infobox; Apple TV lists Shaw and Arntsen). It was the first CGI Miffy. — [Wikipedia, Miffy's Adventures Big and Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy%27s_Adventures_Big_and_Small)
- **[Fact, distributor copy]** Distributor description: 3D CGI for ages 3-6, a peaceful world of "brilliant primary colours" in Bruna's "simple iconic style". — [Studio 100 catalogue](https://www.studio100international.com/en/catalog/miffys-adventures-big-and-small/)
- **[Snippet]** The series composer describes the design as built on a very minimal palette of solid colours. — [David Schweitzer, Miffy](https://www.davidschweitzer.net/miffy/)
- **[Snippet]** Mercis's Marja Kerkhof said Mercis chose Blue-Zoo because it had "a very good understanding of the brand". — [World Screen TV Kids interview](https://worldscreen.com/tvkids/?p=10239) (page returned 403)
- **[Snippet]** Mercis said the show keeps the "same warmth and beautiful atmosphere" of the stop-motion series. — [Animation Magazine, MIPCOM 2014](https://www.animationmagazine.net/2014/10/mipcom-2014-quelque-chose-de-cartoons/)

### Inferences
- Stepped timing (twos and threes) is the principal device Blue-Zoo used to stop CG from looking too slick for Bruna's world. For a 2D part-based Puggle rig the equivalent is holding poses and stepping keys rather than smooth interpolation between them.
- That the client did not notice a mid-series renderer change suggests the look is simple, flat-shaded and solid-coloured enough that lighting subtleties barely register. This is inference, not a stated fact.
- The 2015 series has a voice cast (already established), unlike 2003. No source says how the cross-mouth behaves during speech, so the handling cannot be reported; it would need to be checked by watching episodes.

### Gaps
- Not found anywhere: whether the black outline is a rendered toon line, geometry or texture; whether the face is a texture or modelled; whether the cross animates in speech; whether eyes blink; whether head turns are limited or snapped; any written Mercis rules given to Blue-Zoo. Blue-Zoo's own site, Kidscreen, Skwigly, Broadcast, AWN and Cartoon Brew produced announcements only.
- A Vimeo item titled "Miffy Blue Zoo training" surfaced in search ([vimeo.com/221642062](https://vimeo.com/221642062)) and may show rig or animation-style guidance for animators; it was not viewed, so its content is unknown.
- The Tom Box statements are a blogger's notes from a talk, not a transcript.

## 4. The 2025 series (StudioCanal / Mercis / Superprod)

### Takeaway
Public statements say the look stays and the character and world change: Miffy keeps "the same iconic look" while getting a more complex personality, a contemporary setting and new characters. No technical description of the animation approach has been published that I could find.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker]** StudioCanal's Françoise Guyonnet: Miffy retains the "same iconic look" with a fresh, contemporary vision; she has become a "more complex, funny and engaging character" in a contemporary world. — [Animation Magazine, Oct 2023](https://www.animationmagazine.net/2023/10/new-cg-miffy-series-ready-to-play-with-studiocanal-mercis-superprod/); [AWN](https://www.awn.com/news/studiocanal-mercis-superprod-team-miffy-3dcg-preschool-series) (both via search summaries)
- **[Maker]** Mercis's managing director: completely new stories, new sets and locations, and new characters such as a young brother, Bun. — [Variety, Oct 2023](https://variety.com/2023/tv/global/studiocanal-mercis-superprod-animation-miffy-1235743820/) (via search summary)
- **[Fact]** 78 x 7 minutes, CGI, ages 3-5, produced at Superprod Animation's studios; first episodes due September 2025 for Canal+ and Sky Kids. — [Advanced Television, 27 Feb 2025](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/02/27/studiocanals-miffy-coming-to-sky/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The stated change is in writing (personality, humour, family) rather than in graphic design. Whether the animation is smoother than Blue-Zoo's stepped approach is unknown.

### Gaps
- No review, director interview or studio breakdown of the 2025 series' look was found; all coverage located is announcement press from 2023 to early 2025. Every item in this section is from search summaries, not full-page reads.

## 5. Mercis / Bruna rules on how Miffy may be depicted in animation

### Takeaway
The only explicit rule set on record comes from Gene Deitch, about the 1992 drawn series: characters are always seen en face, must never turn their heads or be seen in profile, and design, colour and animation must match the books with no embellishment. No published Mercis style guide or equivalent statement for the later productions was found.

### Cited Findings
- **[Maker]** Gene Deitch: there were strict graphic rules; the characters "are always seen enface", looking straight into the child viewer's eyes. — [Gene Deitch, "Roll the Credits" ch. 49, Dick Bruna](https://genedeitchcredits.wordpress.com/roll-the-credits/49-dick-bruna/)
- **[Maker]** Deitch: "they must never turn their heads or be seen in profile!" He likens them to graphic icons, as stylised as Kabuki actors. — [Gene Deitch](https://genedeitchcredits.wordpress.com/roll-the-credits/49-dick-bruna/)
- **[Maker]** Deitch: Bruna wanted design, colour and animation to match the books with no "jazzed-up deviations"; only Bruna's own red, yellow, green, blue and brown; all pure flat colour, no textures. — [Gene Deitch](https://genedeitchcredits.wordpress.com/roll-the-credits/49-dick-bruna/)
- **[Maker]** Approval process: Deitch took storyboards to Bruna's Utrecht studio in batches of eight for approval and says "We never had a single rejection." — [Gene Deitch](https://genedeitchcredits.wordpress.com/roll-the-credits/49-dick-bruna/)
- **[Maker]** A practical cost of flat colour on cels: with no texture to hide them, dust and reflections showed, and three or four cel levels dulled the colours, which then had to be corrected scene by scene in grading. — [Gene Deitch](https://genedeitchcredits.wordpress.com/roll-the-credits/49-dick-bruna/)
- **[Snippet]** License Global reports Marja Kerkhof calling the TV series Mercis's biggest commercial challenge, having to satisfy the "rigorous eye" of Miffy's creator; and that when Mercis first sought a 3D adaptation several studios pitched, the results were so disappointing that Mercis stepped back until it could control the process completely. — [License Global, "Dick Bruna"](https://www.licenseglobal.com/licensing-resources/dick-bruna) (page returned 403)

### Inferences
- The en-face rule plainly did not survive unchanged into the puppet and CGI versions in a literal sense, since a critic already described the 2003 series as three-dimensional rather than flat. How strictly the later shows keep the face towards camera is undocumented and would have to be judged by viewing.
- For Puggle the 1992 rules are the closest precedent for a flat part-based rig: frontal face always, no head turn, no profile; movement comes from sliding, bobbing and limb action rather than rotation in depth.

### Gaps
- No Mercis licensing style guide, or reporting on one, was found in English or Dutch. Whether the no-profile rule was formally imposed on Pedri, A. Film, Blue-Zoo or Superprod is unknown.
- Deitch's page does not say how walking was drawn under the no-profile rule, nor how mouths were treated.

## 6. Critical / design-press comparison of book look and animated look

### Takeaway
Very little analytical comparison exists; what there is comes from Dutch film reviews of the 2013 film. They praise colour fidelity and stop-motion's tactility, and note two costs: the puppet versions are three-dimensional rather than flat, and the faces are nearly immobile.

### Cited Findings
- **[Critic]** The TV series is "driedimensionaal", not flat as in Bruna's books. — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Limited facial movement may bother adults, but the target audience "zal het waarschijnlijk niet eens opvallen". — [FilmTotaal](https://www.filmtotaal.nl/recensie/9847)
- **[Critic]** Bruna's primary colours "komen ... goed tot hun recht" in the film. — [Cinemagazine](https://cinemagazine.nl/nijntje-de-film-2013-recensie/)
- **[Maker]** Pedri on what stop-motion adds generally: "het tastbare", bringing tangible lifeless objects to life. — [AV & Entertainment, 2006](https://av-entertainment.nl/lees-artikel/382/Stop-motion-animatie-volop-in-beweging)

### Inferences
- Across thirty years the adaptations moved in one direction: 1992 flat cels with strict frontal rule; 2003 and 2013 real puppets (volume gained, flatness lost, faces still nearly static, speech avoided or played over still faces); 2015 CGI imitating the puppets by stepping on twos and threes. Each step kept solid primary colour and facial stillness and gave up flatness.
- The constant across all versions is restraint in the face. Stillness of the face, not flatness, appears to be what the makers treated as non-negotiable.

### Gaps
- No academic or design-press article comparing the book page with the animated versions was found. Filmkrant, de Volkskrant, NRC and Trouw were not reached within the search budget.
