Puggle

Privacy policy

Last updated: [date it goes live]

Puggle is a baby tracker for families. It's made by [your name, or your business name].

The short version: what you log stays on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. Puggle has no server and no accounts. It doesn't collect, sell or share your information, and it has no adverts or tracking.

What you put in Puggle

You might add:

  • your children's names, birthdays, colours and photos
  • feeds, sleeps, nappies, potty trips, meals, medicines, growth, milestones and notes
  • the names and photos of people in your family
  • reminders

This is kept in two places:

  • On your iPhone. Everything is saved on the phone first, so logging works without a signal.
  • In your own iCloud, using Apple's iCloud service. This keeps your phones in step and lets you share with people you invite. Puggle's developer can't see it. Apple's own privacy policy covers iCloud.

Puggle sends none of it anywhere else.

Sharing with your family

Nobody sees your family's information unless you invite them. Everyone you invite can see the family's entries, and can add to them unless you made them a viewer. You can stop sharing with anyone at any time.

Your Apple Watch, widgets and Lock Screen

Puggle shows timers and the latest entries on your Apple Watch, in widgets, on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. These come from your iPhone and stay on your devices. Anyone who can see your Lock Screen can see these, so there's a setting to show initials there instead of names.

Things Puggle only does if you ask

  • Weather in the picture. If you switch this on, Puggle uses your rough location (to within a few kilometres) to ask Apple's weather service for the weather where you are. Your location isn't saved or shared, and nothing else in the app uses it. It's off until you switch it on.
  • Camera. Used to take a photo of someone in your family, or to read the directions on a medicine box. The directions are read on your iPhone. A photo of a medicine box isn't kept.
  • Photos. Used to choose a photo for someone, or to save a drawing from the play pad.
  • Face ID. Used to unlock Puggle if you switch on its lock. Apple handles Face ID: Puggle never sees your face data.
  • Notifications. Reminders you set are made on your iPhone.
  • Export and import. You can export every entry as a spreadsheet file, or bring in entries from another app's file. What happens to an exported file is up to you.

Backups

Puggle keeps copies of your family's information on your iPhone, so a mistake can be undone. These are part of your iPhone's own iCloud backup if that's on.

What Puggle doesn't do

  • There are no accounts, so Puggle doesn't ask for or keep an email address.
  • There are no adverts, analytics or tracking, and no third-party services that collect data.
  • Puggle doesn't sell or share information with anyone.
  • Puggle gives no medical advice and never suggests a medicine dose.

Test versions

If you test Puggle through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may send the developer crash reports and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. Apple's TestFlight terms cover this. These don't include what you've logged.

Children

Puggle is made for parents and carers to use. It keeps information about children only because you add it, and only in the places above.

Deleting your information

  • Delete an entry or a child in the app. Deleted entries stay in Recently deleted for a while, so they can be brought back.
  • Delete everything from You, Your data. This removes every child and every entry from your iPhone and from iCloud, for everyone in the family.
  • Deleting Puggle from your iPhone removes what's kept on that phone. What's in your iCloud stays until you delete it in the app, or in your iPhone's iCloud settings.

Changes
