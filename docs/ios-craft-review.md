# What makes the best iOS apps feel good: Apollo and others

Date: 11 November 2026. Prepared by a research agent for Puggle.

## Apollo (Reddit client by Christian Selig; launched October 2017, shut down 30 June 2023)

What reviewers singled out (search summaries):

- **Gestures everywhere.** Customisable short and long swipes in both directions on posts, comments and inbox, each with a haptic tick.
- **It looked like Apple made it.** Selig set out to follow Apple's guidelines closely; reviewers called it extremely fast.
- **New system features on day one:** context menus with previews and Dark Mode (iOS 13), seven kinds of widget (iOS 14), Lock Screen widgets and a small pet living on the Dynamic Island (iOS 16).
- **Depth without clutter:** themes, layouts, many alternative app icons including community-made ones.
- **A developer who talked to users** in public throughout.

Pricing (search summaries; some figures conflict between sources):

- Free with no ads (recollection).
- **Pro:** a one-off, about US$4.99 by 2023.
- **Ultra:** a subscription of about US$0.99 a month, covering push notifications, themes and monthly icons. The stated reason was that notifications needed a server with running costs a one-off couldn't cover.
- **Tip jar** in Settings.

Criticised: posting behind the paywall, slow notifications, features ahead of stability, and (the structural lesson) total dependence on someone else's service.

## Other apps

| App | What it is | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Crouton** (read in full) | Recipes; solo developer; Apple Design Award 2024 | Cooking mode shows only the current step. The developer's test: how quickly can I get you back to what you were doing? Much of the work was deciding what to leave out |
| **Flighty** (read in full) | Flight tracking; Apple Design Award 2023 | Live Activities modelled on departure boards, one line each. Designed for the connection dropping. The goal was "boringly obvious". Its high price and paywalled Live Activities are not to copy |
| **Gentler Streak** (read in full) | Fitness; Apple Design Award 2024 | A mascot that guides without pushing, and progress framed against your own history, not other people's |
| **Bears Gratitude** (read in full) | Journal; two-person team; Apple Design Award 2024 | No sign-in screen: straight into the experience. Mascots as a way into the content |
| **Timery** | Time tracking | Start and stop timers from a widget without opening the app; a Live Activity follows. The closest match to a feed or sleep timer |
| **Carrot Weather** | Weather with a personality | The personality is a dial with levels, and can be turned off |
| **Things 3** | Tasks | Adding and completing items is frictionless; one-off purchase |
| **Streaks** | Habits | Simplicity held as the core rule through every version |
| **Overcast** | Podcasts | Generous free app with an optional small yearly payment |

## Baby trackers: the bar today (search summaries, untested)

- No baby tracker was found with editorial praise for native craft. That is the opening.
- **Nara Baby** is described as clean and calm, free and ad-free, with widgets, watch access and Live Activities for sleep, pump and feed timers.
- **Huckleberry** is very highly rated but criticised for putting sleep predictions behind a costly subscription.
- Several newer independent trackers advertise Lock Screen and Dynamic Island timers, Siri and watch support.
- The common one-star complaints across the category: sync failing between parents, lost data, and paywalls.

So Lock Screen timers are now expected. Reliability, sync and restraint are where others fall short.

## Patterns that recur

1. Swipe actions on every row, with a haptic tick.
2. Long-press menus on every object, destructive actions last.
3. A Live Activity for anything with a running clock.
4. Acting without opening the app: widgets, Lock Screen, Siri and Shortcuts.
5. Showing only the current step.
6. Leaving features out.
7. Apple's conventions first; custom design only at the signature moment.
8. Adopting new system features on day one.
9. Personality as an adjustable layer, never in the way.
10. Deep customisation, tucked away.
11. No sign-in wall; value in the first seconds.
12. Honest pricing, with the reason stated.

## Recommendations for Puggle, ranked for one-handed use at 3 am

1. **A Live Activity for every running timer** (feed, sleep, pump) with stop buttons. Apple's guidance is that these last about eight hours, so a long night sleep needs a fallback.
2. **One-tap logging from Home and Lock Screen widgets,** without opening the app (iOS 17).
3. **Build every log action as an App Intent first.** One piece of work then gives Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight, the Action button and widgets.
4. **A thumb-zone Today:** big log buttons at the bottom, sheets that swipe away, sensible defaults, undo in place of confirmation.
5. **Sparing, distinct haptics** so start, stop and saved can be felt without looking.
6. **Swipe actions and long-press menus on every history row.**
7. **A true night mode:** very dim, no white flash on launch.
8. **Treat sync between two parents as the craft feature.** It is the category's top complaint. A timer appearing on the other parent's Lock Screen would need a server, which Puggle avoids today.
9. **Apollo's pricing honesty:** all logging free for ever, a one-off for extras, a tip jar, and never a paywall on widgets, Siri or Lock Screen timers.
10. **Puggle as a quiet layer** that can be turned down, with Apple's built-in tips to teach gestures in place of an onboarding tour.
