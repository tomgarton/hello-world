WHERE THE LINE IS

Three kinds of help, and what each is safe for

Patterns and sentencesPlain arithmetic on the entries, and sentences filled in from fixed wording. No model is involved, it works on every iPhone, and the same entries always give the same answer. Screens 1 to 3 are this.
Reading from trusted sourcesA short list of links, chosen by subject and the child's age, to pages such as the NHS's. The app shows the title and opens the page. It doesn't quote, shorten or reword them, so the advice stays theirs.
A model on the phoneGood at turning entries into a readable paragraph and at answering "when did" and "how often". It can also state things that are not in the entries, so every answer shows the entries behind it, and it stays away from what a parent should do.

What I would not build: tips or recommendations written by the app. "Ollie's naps are short, try an earlier bedtime" is advice about one child's health from software that knows only what was logged. A small on-device model will say such things fluently whether or not they are right. It is also the point where an app can start to count as a medical device under the rules in the UK, the EU and Australia. I am not a lawyer and that needs checking before anything like it ships.

What does the same job safely: the app says what changed, and a trusted source says what it might mean. "Bedtime has moved about an hour later this fortnight" with "NHS: Helping your baby to sleep" underneath gives a parent the fact and somewhere reliable to take it.

Sources by country. The NHS suits the UK. Elsewhere the list would need its own, such as Raising Children Network in Australia. Linking is fine; reusing their words inside the app has terms attached, which is another reason to link.

On-device models. Apple's own on-device model is only on recent iPhones with Apple Intelligence switched on, so screen 4 would be missing for many families. Screens 1 to 3 need none of it. What that model can do reliably with a few thousand entries needs testing on a real phone before I would promise it.

The earlier rule. Insights was totals and counts only. This keeps "no targets and no normal ranges" and lets go of "no averages", for a child's own history. "Just logging" still hides all of it.
