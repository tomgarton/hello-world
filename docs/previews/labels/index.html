The same note in the app

On the bottle sheet until it is dismissed or the day has passed, with the two rows either side of the change. It goes for good on "Got it".

It needs the ages as numbers. At the checking step, each row's starting age is confirmed along with the amounts, so the app knows that a row starts at 6 months. A row it can't place, such as one by weight, gets no notice.

Formula first. A tin's table is by age and used every day, so the notice is plainly useful. For a medicine it would only fire for one with a regular reminder set, so a box in the cupboard never sends one.

It follows the reminder rules: silent, on this iPhone, off with one switch, and nothing at all in "Just logging".

The wording never gives the amount. "The tin has a different row from 6 months" is a fact about the tin. "Ollie now needs 7 scoops" would be the app deciding, and babies do not all move up on the day.

WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

Typed up, but never trusted blind

A misread digit is the risk. A reader that turns 2.5 into 25 is worse than no reader. So nothing it reads is saved until a person has ticked each row against the photo, and the photo is kept so the check can be made again.

The table says what the box says. The app copies it out and does no sums: nothing by weight, nothing worked out for a child's age.

The child's age is shown, and no row is highlighted. Picking out "the row for Ollie" would be the app choosing a dose, and ages on a box often come with conditions the app can't read. Showing the age beside the table lets the parent read across. This is the one I would most like your view on.

Where a table can't be read, the photo is kept on its own with the name, and the rules can be typed by hand as now.

Boxes change. Each saved table carries the date it was read, and "Read a new box" replaces it.
