The shape heap, and things to hand a child

The "building your family" screen redone as you described: big shapes that fall across the whole screen and pile up at the bottom, and that you can push around. Then a sketch of a high-contrast view for young babies, and other quiet distractions. All of it is a sketch; nothing is built.

PART 1

Building your family: the heap

Fourteen large shapes drop in and pile up. Five carry the family's initials. Drag any shape to throw it, or move the tilt slider to tip the phone.

How it would work in the app

  • The phone's own tilt moves the heap, through its motion sensors. Tip it and the shapes slide and tumble; turn it over and they fall to the other end.
  • Touch works too: drag, flick and poke. With a soft tap from the phone when shapes land, if haptics are on.
  • The button wakes once the shapes have landed, about two seconds in. Nobody has to wait, and a parent in a hurry can tap straight through.
  • Real outlines. This sketch treats every shape as round, so they overlap a little and stack loosely. The app would use each shape's true outline, so they would lock together more like blocks.
  • Reduce Motion shows the finished heap, still.
  • No sensor permission is needed for tilt on iPhone.
PART 2

For a young baby: high contrast

Black, white and red shapes that drift slowly across a dark screen. Newborns pick out strong contrast most easily, which is why these three colours are used in books and cards for them.

What I'd hold to

  • Slow, and never flashing. Nothing blinks, strobes or changes suddenly. Shapes only glide and turn.
  • No claims. The app would call it something to look at, and would not say it helps development. That keeps to the rule of no medical advice.
  • It ends itself. A parent picks two, five or ten minutes, then it fades to a still picture. No count, no streak, nothing kept.
  • Screen dimmed, sound off unless the parent turns a soft sound on.
  • Held behind the cover the play pad already uses, so a stray hand can't leave it.

Things to decide before building any of it

  • Does it belong in this app? It makes the app more loved by the child and more useful on a hard day. It also moves it away from "a calm tracker for the parent". One "Play" place, off the main screen, keeps both.
  • Everything works with no signal, since a plane is one of the main places for it.
  • No guilt. No totals of time spent, no reminders to play, nothing in Insights.
  • Getting out. The cover needs a way out a parent can find and a child can't: the hold-to-leave the play pad uses, and a note about Guided Access in Help.
  • Free or paid. This would be a natural part of a paid tier later, if you want one.
