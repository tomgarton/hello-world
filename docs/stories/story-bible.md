# Puggle and friends: story bible

First draft, 16 November 2026. Everything here is a proposal for Tom to change. It builds on the research in
`reports/Picture books for under fives.md` and on what is already settled about the cast in
`docs/baby-tracker-spec.md` and the brand book. Where this draft changes something settled, it says so.

Tom's choices so far (15 November 2026): the first stories are for toddlers of 2 to 4; the first book is a
lift-the-flap search; the voice is plain prose with a refrain; the friends are neighbours along the creek.

## What the stories are for

Small, ordinary days in the life of a toddler, told at her height, for a grown-up and a child to read
together. A story is finished in three or four minutes and is worth reading again tomorrow.

What they do not do: teach letters or numbers, state a moral, or set anything to beat. If a story has a
lesson, the child finds it in what happens. This is the same promise the app makes to parents.

## The engine

**Puggle wants something small, right now, and the creek gets in the way.**

A boot, a turn, a stick, to be first, to stay up, to do it herself. The want is one a two-year-old really
has. What gets in the way is ordinary: a baby sister, a friend who wants the same thing, mud, bedtime. The
story ends when the want is met, or has changed into something better, and she is home.

Ten springboards, to show the engine runs:

1. One boot is missing.
2. Pebble has the thing Puggle wants, and will not let go.
3. Puggle wants to carry the bucket by herself. It is full.
4. Everyone can cross the stepping stones but her.
5. Kip is coming to play, and is taking a very long time.
6. Digby has dug a hole exactly where the game was.
7. Puggle does not want a bath. The creek is a bath.
8. Nan's house has different rules, and Puggle knows them better than Pop does.
9. Quill needs quiet. Puggle has a new whistle.
10. It is raining, and the puddle is the best one yet, and it is time to go in.

## The world

**The creek.** One slow, shallow creek with a sandy bend, and five homes a child can find on a map. The
places have plain names, as a child would give them. Nothing is named from a language list, and no place
is a real place.

| Place | Who lives there | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| **The burrow** | Puggle, Pebble, Mum and Dad | In the bank by the still pool, with a round door. Every story starts or ends here. |
| **The log pile** | Quill and family | Up the bank, dry and quiet, full of small gaps. The place for making and sorting. |
| **The big gum** | Kip and family | The tallest tree, by the bridge. You can see the whole creek from the top, if you are allowed up. |
| **The paddock** | Digby and family | Past the fence, all soft dirt, with more doors than anyone has counted. |
| **The sandy bend** | Sunny and family | Where the creek goes wide and warm. The place for running and for picnics. |
| **The stepping stones** | Nan and Pop, on the far side | Seven stones across the creek. Their house has a porch, a kettle and looser rules. |

A map of the creek is the first picture in every book, the same each time, with the homes in the same
places. It is also the natural busy scene to explore.

**World rules.**

- The pictures are at a toddler's height. Grown-ups are often legs and hands.
- A grown-up never solves the problem. They can be warm, be busy, join the game, and get it wrong.
- Play is real while it lasts. If Puggle says the log is a boat, the picture shows a boat.
- Nothing frightening happens that is not put right on the next page.
- No shops, screens, money or brands. Nobody is told off.
- The animals are animals who live as families do. They do not eat each other and nobody is "wild".
- Every story ends at home, on the same last line: **"And the creek went on by."**
- One small thing is hidden in every picture for a child to find: **a little paper boat.** (The series' own;
  a child who cannot read can find it faster than the grown-up.)

## The format

- **Age:** 2 to 4, read by a grown-up with the child. Nothing is made for a child under two to use alone.
- **Length:** 8 to 12 pictures. Under 200 words for a flap book; up to about 350 for a story.
- **Voice:** a narrator, in the present tense, in short plain sentences, with one line that comes back.
  Written for a grown-up's mouth and read aloud before it is kept.
- **Speech:** the cast has no drawn mouths, and Puggle has only a handful of words. The narrator tells most
  of it. A line of speech is short and is something a toddler would say.
- **One thing to do on each picture:** lift one flap, or find the boat, or say the refrain. A flap is always
  the answer to the words, never decoration. No games, no sounds that are not in the story.
- **Sound:** none unless the grown-up turns it on. No narration to begin with: the grown-up is the voice.

## The cast

Each profile has the same parts: who they are in a line, age, look, what they want, their soft spot, what
is hard for them at this age, what they always do, how they sound, where they live, how they are when
things go wrong, and what they never do. Ages are spaced so that each child wants, and can do, something
different in the same game.

**Three things in this draft change what the spec has now, for Tom to decide.** Kip was "can fall asleep
anywhere", Sunny was "always smiling", and Digby was "the potty jokes". The sleepy koala and the smiling
quokka are the stock versions of those animals, and koala and wombat are the two most crowded animals in
Australian children's books. So each friend here has a want and a skill in place of a habit or a fault.
The names, animals and looks are unchanged.

### Puggle

- **In a line:** she wants to do it herself, now.
- **Age:** 2½. Platypus.
- **Look:** as drawn: the lick of hair, the flower clip, a pocket. In the stories she is nearly always in or
  near water.
- **Wants:** to do what the bigger ones do, and to be first.
- **Soft spot:** Pebble. She is fierce about her and jealous of her, sometimes on the same page.
- **Hard at this age:** waiting, sharing, and stopping.
- **Always does:** puts things in her pocket. Whatever is in the pocket matters by the end.
- **Sounds like:** a few words used hard. "Me do it." "Again." "Mine." "Uh oh."
- **Home:** the burrow.
- **When it goes wrong:** she goes still, then loud, then needs a lap. It passes quickly.
- **Never:** is mean on purpose, and is never called naughty.

### Pebble

- **In a line:** the baby, who is busy with her own plans.
- **Age:** 6 months at the start, and she grows: sitting, then crawling, then standing, across the books.
- **Look:** as drawn, with the curl on top.
- **Wants:** whatever Puggle is holding.
- **Can do, now:** sit up, grab, drop, bang, laugh, put it in her bill. Cannot crawl yet, so she cannot
  follow, and things come to her.
- **Always does:** drops the thing at the worst moment, and is delighted.
- **Sounds like:** one sound, "Ba!", which means everything. Puggle is the one who knows what it means.
- **In a story:** she interrupts. Each interruption sends the game somewhere new, and the last one makes it
  better.
- **Never:** is a problem to be got rid of.

### Mum

- **In a line:** in the middle of something, and glad to be interrupted.
- **Look:** as drawn.
- **Wants:** five minutes with her tea while it is hot. Rarely gets them.
- **Is good at:** making a game out of what has to be done anyway.
- **Honest small feeling:** tired.
- **Sounds like:** "Shall we?" and "Nearly."
- **Never:** fixes it for her, or gives a speech.

### Dad

- **In a line:** says yes to the game, then finds out what he has agreed to.
- **Look:** as drawn.
- **Wants:** to get the washing in before it rains.
- **Is good at:** being the boat, the bridge, the monster and the horse.
- **Honest small feeling:** distracted; he has lost the thing he was holding a moment ago.
- **Sounds like:** "Righto." and "One more, then."
- **Never:** is the fool. He is playing, and the child knows it.

Mum and Dad do the same jobs in the same number of stories: the bath, the cooking, the mending, the work,
the comforting. Counted across the series, not left to chance.

### Nan

- **In a line:** has all the time in the world and a tin of something.
- **Look:** as drawn, with her reading glasses on the top of her bill.
- **Wants:** to be shown how. Puggle teaches Nan the game, and Nan gets it a little wrong on purpose.
- **Is good at:** mending, growing things, and not hurrying.
- **Sounds like:** "Show me." and "Well, I never."
- **House rule:** at Nan's you can have the second biscuit.

### Pop

- **In a line:** knows where everything on the creek is, and what it used to be.
- **Look:** as drawn, with his round glasses.
- **Wants:** company while he potters.
- **Is good at:** knots, whistling, naming birds, and fixing things with string.
- **Sounds like:** "Hold this for me." and a whistle of two notes that means he is coming.
- **Never:** tells a long story when a short one will do.

### Quill

- **In a line:** the maker, who likes things just so.
- **Age:** 3½. Echidna.
- **Look:** as drawn. Carries a small bag of useful bits.
- **Wants:** to finish what she is making before anyone touches it.
- **Is good at:** building, sorting, and noticing what is missing.
- **Soft spot:** noise and rush. When it is too much she curls up, and comes back when it is quiet.
- **Hard at this age:** someone else doing it differently.
- **Always does:** lines things up.
- **Sounds like:** "Not yet." and "There."
- **Home:** the log pile.
- **Never:** is called shy. She is busy.

*Changed from the spec:* she was "gets prickly when tired". Curling up when it is too much keeps that idea,
and it is hers to decide when to uncurl.

### Kip

- **In a line:** the climber, who sees what is coming before anyone else.
- **Age:** 3. Koala. Puggle's best friend.
- **Look:** as drawn.
- **Wants:** to go higher, and to tell everyone what he can see from there.
- **Is good at:** climbing, holding on, and spotting things far away.
- **Soft spot:** the ground when it is wet. He does not like the creek on his fur.
- **Hard at this age:** coming down, and going at anyone else's pace. Kip is slow and cannot be hurried.
- **Always does:** arrives last, having seen something on the way.
- **Sounds like:** "I can see..." and "Wait for me."
- **Home:** the big gum.
- **Never:** falls asleep as the joke.

*Changed from the spec:* he was "can fall asleep anywhere", which is every koala in every book. Slow and
high keeps him gentle and makes him the opposite of Puggle, who is fast and low and wet. He can still be
the one in the sleep stories: he is the last to come down for bed.

### Digby

- **In a line:** the youngest of the friends, who does whatever Puggle does, a moment later and messier.
- **Age:** 2. Wombat.
- **Look:** as drawn. Always has dirt on him somewhere.
- **Wants:** to be in it, whatever it is.
- **Is good at:** digging, pushing, and finding things underground that nobody knew were lost.
- **Soft spot:** being left behind.
- **Hard at this age:** being careful. He goes through things, not round them.
- **Always does:** comes up out of the ground in the wrong place, pleased to see everyone.
- **Sounds like:** "Me too!" and "Dig dig dig."
- **Home:** the paddock.
- **Never:** is stubborn or greedy, which are the stock wombat traits.

*Changed from the spec:* he was "the potty jokes". He can still be in the potty stories as the one who is
not there yet and wants to be, which is kinder and truer for a two-year-old than being the joke.

### Sunny

- **In a line:** the big one, who knows the rules of every game because she has just made them up.
- **Age:** 4½. Quokka.
- **Look:** as drawn.
- **Wants:** everyone to play her game, her way, and for it to be fair.
- **Is good at:** hopping, counting to ten with some numbers missing, and being in charge.
- **Soft spot:** when the little ones will not follow the rules, and when she is the one who gets it wrong.
- **Hard at this age:** losing, and letting somebody else be the leader.
- **Always does:** says how the game goes before it starts.
- **Sounds like:** "The rule is..." and "That's not fair." and, when it works, "Told you."
- **Home:** the sandy bend.
- **Never:** smiles as her whole character.

*Changed from the spec:* she was "always smiling", the stock quokka. As the oldest she gives the stories
someone to look up to and someone to push against, and she can still be the one who leads the cheering in
the milestone stories.

### Telling them apart

| | Animal | Age | Is good at | Says | Lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Puggle | Platypus | 2½ | Water, and trying | "Me do it." | The burrow |
| Pebble | Platypus | 6 months | Grabbing and dropping | "Ba!" | The burrow |
| Digby | Wombat | 2 | Digging | "Me too!" | The paddock |
| Kip | Koala | 3 | Climbing and seeing far | "I can see..." | The big gum |
| Quill | Echidna | 3½ | Making and sorting | "Not yet." | The log pile |
| Sunny | Quokka | 4½ | Rules and hopping | "The rule is..." | The sandy bend |

Two girls and two boys among the friends as drawn; the skills are spread so that the maker and the leader
are girls and the youngest and the gentlest are boys. Worth a count again whenever a story is added.

## The first stories

Twelve outlines, each a different shape from the research, so the series can find which ones suit it.
The first is written out in `one-boot.md`.

| | Title | Shape | Who | In a line | The line that comes back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One Boot** | Lift-the-flap search | Everyone | Puggle has one boot on and goes along the creek to find the other. | "One boot on. One boot gone." |
| 2 | **Ba!** | Baby interrupts | Puggle, Pebble, Mum | Puggle builds a dam. Pebble wants each thing she builds it with. The last thing Pebble drops is what holds it. | "Ba!" said Pebble. |
| 3 | **Seven Stones** | Try, wobble, helper | Puggle, Pop, Kip | Everyone can cross the stepping stones but Puggle. She gets one stone further each try, and gets wet each time, which she does not mind. | "Splash. Again." |
| 4 | **Me Do It** | A first experience, step by step | Puggle, Dad | Getting dressed for the rain, each thing put on wrong and put right, by her. | "Me do it." |
| 5 | **Wait for Kip** | Chant with a question | Puggle, Kip, the friends | Everyone is ready to play. Where is Kip? Each page finds what he stopped to look at. | "Where is Kip? Kip is coming." |
| 6 | **The Rule Is** | Game with a snag | Sunny, Puggle, Digby, Quill | Sunny's game has a rule for everything. Digby cannot keep any of them. The game only works when a rule is made for Digby. | "The rule is..." |
| 7 | **Not Yet** | Offer and not quite | Quill, the friends | Quill is making something and needs one more piece. Each friend brings one. Each is nearly right. | "Not yet," said Quill. |
| 8 | **Dig Dig Dig** | Three and back again | Digby, Puggle | Digby digs to three wrong places looking for his own front door, then back through all three the other way. | "Dig dig dig. Up he comes." |
| 9 | **Bath** | A first experience, with a turn | Puggle, Mum | Puggle will not have a bath. She will, though, swim, splash, dive and float, and is surprised to be clean. | "No bath." |
| 10 | **Show Me** | The grandparent is taught | Puggle, Nan | Puggle shows Nan how to make a paper boat. Nan gets each fold a little wrong. | "Show me," said Nan. |
| 11 | **Along the Creek** | A busy scene to explore | Everyone | The map, come to life: one big picture for each home, things to name, small things going on, the paper boat to find. No story. | None. |
| 12 | **Goodnight, Creek** | A quiet sequence | Everyone, one at a time | Each home at dusk, one picture each, getting quieter, ending in the burrow. | "And the creek went on by." |

Hard things, when they come (a new baby, a goodbye, a visit to the doctor), are told in the usual format:
one fact on a page, and the feeling named.
