# Puggle storyworld: environment bible (working v0.1)

## 1. Creative intent

A small contemporary community with everyday domestic life and direct access to nature. The animals **dress and live in houses like people**. The setting is warm and recognisable, with a little discovery just beyond a garden gate. It should not look like a magical woodland village, nor a realistic wildlife documentary.

**Status key:** `[ESTABLISHED]` from original artwork/user instructions; `[DIRECTION]` positively received; `[PROPOSAL]` created in this exploratory iteration; `[OPEN]` still undecided.

## 2. Existing app scene language

- `[ESTABLISHED]` **Four outdoor scene families:** hills, beach, backyard, bush. Day part variations: dawn, day, dusk, night, dark look. See `puggle_outdoor_scenes.jpeg`.
- `[ESTABLISHED]` **Four rooms:** tea table, living room, playroom, bedroom. Evening/night variants. See `puggle_interior_rooms.png`.
- `[ESTABLISHED]` **World shapes are outline-free**; characters have prescribed outlines. This distinction is essential.
- `[ESTABLISHED]` Rounded hills and trees, restrained props, broad colour fields, flat simple clouds, and cosy colour shifts between day and night.
- `[ESTABLISHED]` Interior details include mustard curtains and pendant in the tea-table space; a blue sofa and television in the living room; bunting, blocks and a shelf in the playroom; and lilac bedroom walls, blue bedding and a mobile.
- `[DIRECTION]` Expand these shapes, not the rendering style: more architectural specificity is permissible as long as scenery stays readable at picture-book scale.

## 3. The places

### A. Puggle's house — start and finish point of many stories

- `[DIRECTION]` Proper, contemporary single-storey family house, family garden, toys, boots near the door. One exterior, many interior rooms; do not present it as a burrow.
- `[DIRECTION]` The liked exterior concept shows cream walls, coral/terracotta pitched roof, arched coral door, round upper window, mustard curtains, white picket gate, washing line, big tree, rolling hills and a distant water view. These are **concept features**, not irrevocable construction details.
- `[PROPOSAL]` Practical domestic visual anchors: boot bench/area at entrance, visible kitchen beyond door, toy ball by the garden fence.
- `[OPEN]` Should the house's pitched attic read as inhabitable? If not, adjust roof/proportions to keep it plausibly single-storey.

### B. Garden and connecting path

- `[ESTABLISHED]` Existing backyard includes pale picket fence, clothesline and a snail.
- `[PROPOSAL]` Gate opens onto a smooth winding cream path; log pile, digging patch and tree form a connected route rather than three unrelated hiding places.
- `[PROPOSAL]` A slice of Puggle's coral-roofed house is visible from the path; Nan and Pop's veranda can appear farther away. This lets children recognise where they've come from.
- `[OPEN]` Define fences and distances after storyboard blocking; no unsupported precise map.

### C. Nan and Pop's house

- `[PROPOSAL]` Cream weatherboard home with desaturated green metal roof, green door, shaded front veranda, rocking chair, mustard curtains, vegetable beds, potted plants and outdoor picnic table.
- `[PROPOSAL]` Biscuit tin belongs here; towel may be on the line or garden table; Pop's hat could sit near the outdoor chair, with Nan nearby. Details follow the manuscript without requiring additional dialogue.
- `[OPEN]` Character staging of Nan/Pop, and whether a hat/table appear in the same view, must be tested for readable flap placement.

### D. The creek

- `[DIRECTION]` A shallow, safe-looking waterway accessible on foot from home. Outdoor scenes should feel connected to the existing hills/bush backgrounds.
- `[PROPOSAL]` Paperbark-like light trunks, simple rounded foliage, flat blue water, smooth rocks, low stepping stones, small pale bridge, quiet picnic patch with basket and blanket.
- `[PROPOSAL]` Sound is optional and restrained. Keep the creek visually calm, not a dramatic river crossing.
- `[OPEN]` Is the footbridge a recurring landmark, or does it crowd the final *One Boot* reveal? The last page should prioritise Puggle's splash.

### E. Village centre

- `[PROPOSAL]` Several individual houses, one modest shop with a sunny awning, a small street and planted front gardens. Homes are varied but belong to the same local architecture family.
- `[OPEN]` No formal village name or geography fixed. The village is **not visited** in *One Boot*, but is useful for later books.

## 4. Location continuity for One Boot

The proposed traversal, not literal walking-time geometry:

```
Puggle's front door / boot area
  -> home garden
  -> log pile (Quill, cone)
  -> digging patch / hole (Digby, flower)
  -> tree (Kip, leaf)
  -> Nan and Pop's garden or nearby home
  -> towel (Sunny, bucket)
  -> Pop's hat (duck; Nan's remark)
  -> tin (biscuit)
  -> squelchy walk to creek picnic spot
  -> basket / blanket (Pebble and the boot)
  -> Puggle's response
  -> creek splash
```

This is **visual connective tissue** added to the existing story, not new manuscript text. The small village high street is off-route.

## 5. World art rules and image treatment

- Environment: no dark outlines or comic outlines around houses/trees/props; simple filled shapes, clean geometry, restrained texture. (Some generated study imagery has incidental thin borders, which should be removed in production.)
- Characters: the opposite rule — exact silhouettes and approved darker outlines; do not redraw them from loose description.
- No photoreal shading or painterly brush effects in printed story illustrations.
- Texture for the eventual digital book belongs chiefly to **physical board material**, not to flat world artwork.
- A leaf or log can become an independent die-cut flap shape. The revealed artwork must still work when read with no animation.
- Keep critical objects large and spatially unambiguous: missing boot, single cone, single flower, leaf, bucket, duck, biscuit, basket and blanket.
- Keep enough quiet negative space for text or reserve the facing page for text. Don't superimpose long text on busy environmental detail.

## 6. Colour and time of day

The original app scenes are canonical for **relative palette and lighting**. Do not infer official hex tokens from screenshots. Day: pale blue sky, soft green hills and bright but not neon props. Dawn/dusk: peach/lilac sky. Night: deep indigo with simplified lit windows and warm pools of light. The newly generated location board explores a brighter daytime palette; if it feels louder than the original, bring it back towards the original app sheet.

## 7. Props that become world landmarks

- Home: front door, boot area, white gate, washing line.
- Backyard/path: logs, hole, big tree, flowers, snail.
- Grandparents: shade/veranda, hat, biscuit tin, towel, outdoor table.
- Creek: picnic blanket, basket, stones, gentle blue water.
- Village: shop awning, small homes and gardens.

## 8. Open art-direction decisions (do not silently settle)

1. Official neighbourhood/village name, or whether it remains unnamed.
2. Exterior house architecture and colour — coral roof is liked but not locked.
3. Which side of the house the creek sits on; map is schematic.
4. How much of the village appears as scenery in stories, versus a destination.
5. Which flora is genuinely regional versus stylised; consult a nature reference if exact ecology matters.
6. Whether exterior scenes should have day/dusk/night variants immediately or only as needed for books.
7. Whether the first board-book prototype uses the log flap or the recessed digging patch as its lead demonstration.
