# One Boot

A lift-the-flap search for ages 2 to 4. First draft, 16 November 2026, for Tom to change.

**In a line:** Puggle has one yellow boot on. She goes along the creek to find the other, and each friend
she asks comes along.

**Why this one first.** It is a search with a flap on each picture, which is what Tom chose. It walks the
whole creek, so it introduces every home and every friend in one book. And the want is one a two-year-old
has every morning.

**How it is its own.** The search is outdoors, along one path, and nobody ever says "no": every flap finds
a friend and something yellow that is not the boot, and the friend joins in. By the end there is a parade.
The boot was never lost. Pebble has had it all along.

**Shape.** Eleven pictures. One flap on each of pictures 2 to 9. About 190 words. The line that comes back
is said by the grown-up and, by the third time, by the child.

**To find on every picture:** the little paper boat.

## The words

Each picture has its words, what is drawn, and what the flap is. Words in bold are the line that comes back.

**1. The burrow, morning.**
Puggle at the round door, one yellow boot on, one bare foot. Mum behind, with tea.

> It is a puddle day.
> Puggle has her boots.
> **One boot on. One boot gone.**

**2. The log pile.** *Flap: the top log.*

> Is it under the logs?

Under the flap: Quill, with a banksia cone, lining cones up in a row.

> No boot. A cone! And Quill.
> "Not yet," says Quill. Then she comes too.
> **One boot on. One boot gone.**

**3. The paddock.** *Flap: a mound of dirt.*

> Is it down the hole?

Under the flap: Digby, coming up out of the ground with a yellow flower on his head.

> No boot. A flower! And Digby.
> "Me too!" says Digby.
> **One boot on. One boot gone.**

**4. The big gum.** *Flap: a bunch of leaves, high up.*

> Is it up the tree?

Under the flap: Kip, holding on, with one yellow leaf.

> No boot. A leaf! And Kip.
> "I can see the whole creek," says Kip. "Wait for me."

**5. The sandy bend.** *Flap: a striped towel on the sand.*

> Is it under the towel?

Under the flap: Sunny, with a yellow bucket.

> No boot. A bucket! And Sunny.
> "The rule is," says Sunny, "we look together."
> **One boot on. One boot gone.**

**6. The stepping stones.** *Flap: Pop's hat, on the last stone.*
The five of them in a line across the stones, Puggle wobbling in one boot, Kip last.

> Is it under Pop's hat?

Under the flap: a small yellow duckling, which has been sitting there.

> No boot. A duck!
> "Well, I never," says Nan.

**7. Nan and Pop's porch.** *Flap: the lid of the biscuit tin.*

> Is it in the tin?

Under the flap: biscuits, and one for everybody.

> No boot. A biscuit.
> Two, at Nan's.

**8. The way home.** No flap. One wide picture: the whole parade walking back along the creek, in order of
size, each carrying their yellow thing. Puggle at the front, one boot, one muddy foot.

> Step, squelch. Step, squelch.
> **One boot on. One boot gone.**

**9. The burrow, the basket by the door.** *Flap: the blanket.*

> Is it in the basket?

Under the flap: Pebble, sitting up, with the yellow boot on her head.

> Ba!

**10. The same, closer.** No flap. Puggle reaches for the boot. Pebble holds on with both hands. Everybody
waits. Then Puggle takes off her one boot, and puts it on her own head.

> Puggle looks at the boot.
> Pebble looks at Puggle.
> "Mine," says Puggle. Then: "Ba."

**11. Outside the burrow.** No flap. Puggle and Pebble with a boot each on their heads, the friends with a
cone, a flower, a leaf and a bucket on theirs, Dad coming with the washing and looking twice. The puddle,
untouched, in front. Then two bare feet in it.

> No boots on.
> Splash.
> And the creek went on by.

## Notes for making it

- **A flap is the answer.** The words ask, the flap answers. Nothing else on the picture can be tapped,
  except the paper boat, which gives a small wobble and nothing more.
- **Big flaps.** Each flap is at least 2 cm across on the smallest phone, and none sits near the bottom edge.
- **A flap can be shut and opened again.** Opening it is the fun, so it never locks open.
- **No dead ends.** The page can be turned whether or not the flap has been lifted.
- **No narration at first.** The grown-up reads. If a voice is added later it is a setting.
- **Colour.** Yellow is Wattle, and it is kept for the boot and the five yellow things, so a child can find
  them. The paper boat is white.
- **The parade grows:** one, then two, three, four, five, and the duckling follows from picture 6. A child
  who looks will see it has come all the way home.
- **Order of homes** is the order on the map, walking downstream and back, so the map and the book agree.

## To check

- Read it aloud to a two-year-old and a three-year-old, and note where they look away.
- Whether picture 10 is clear without its words. If a child needs it explained, the picture is wrong.
- Whether "Mine", then "Ba", is enough for the turn. The idea is that Puggle answers in Pebble's word.
- Each friend's first line here is their line from the bible. If a profile changes, change it here.
